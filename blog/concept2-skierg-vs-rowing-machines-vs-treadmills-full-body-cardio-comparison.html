Concept2 SkiErg vs. Rowing Machines vs. Treadmills: Full-Body Cardio Comparison (2026)

The fitness equipment market is crowded with machines promising full-body cardio work, but they deliver fundamentally different experiences. If you're deciding between a Concept2 SkiErg, a traditional rower, or sticking with a treadmill, the choice hinges on movement pattern, joint impact, learning curve, space, and budget—not hype.

The SkiErg mimics cross-country skiing and costs $1,900 and up. Concept2 rowing machines run $500–$1,200 used and are widely available. Treadmills span $300–$3,000 depending on features. Each recruits different muscle groups and demands different things from your body. This guide cuts through marketing claims to show you what each machine actually delivers, who benefits most, and where you might be overpaying.

Concept2 SkiErg with PM5 Monitor

A diagonal-pattern ski ergometer that engages shoulders, back, core, and legs simultaneously without ground impact. The included PM5 monitor tracks splits, distance, calories, and power output with commercial-grade precision, integrating with Concept2's digital logbook for long-term progress tracking. Dimensions roughly 6.5–7 feet long and 2 feet wide; weighs 85 pounds. Build quality uses sealed bearings, powder-coated steel frame, and minimal plastic—designed for years of reliable use in commercial and home settings.

Best for: Athletes seeking upper-body and core engagement without joint impact; people with knee or ankle issues who want intense cardio; home gym owners with 7×2 feet of floor space who value precision data and durability over budget.

Concept2 Rowing Machine (Model D or E)

A horizontal pulling motion that engages legs, back, core, and arms in sequence. Used models available for $500–$800; new machines range $900–$1,200. Includes PM5 monitor on current models. Smaller footprint than SkiErg in some dimensions (roughly 8 feet long but narrower). Proven durability with decades of commercial and home use. Rowers have a shallower learning curve than SkiErg—most people achieve effective technique within 2–3 sessions rather than 2–3 weeks.

Best for: Budget-conscious buyers seeking full-body cardio; people wanting a lower learning curve; those who can find used inventory; athletes comfortable with traditional pulling mechanics; home gyms with tight budgets under $1,000.

Treadmill (Mid-Range, $500–$1,500)

Vertical impact-based cardio focusing primarily on legs and cardiovascular system. No upper-body recruitment. Familiar movement pattern requires zero learning curve. Footprint varies widely but typically 6–7 feet long and 3+ feet deep. Impact on knees, hips, and ankles limits consecutive-day tolerance for many users. Ideal for steady-state aerobic work and familiar to most fitness users.

Best for: Budget buyers; people prioritizing familiar movement patterns; runners training for running-specific goals; users without knee/hip concerns; those wanting straightforward, low-learning-curve cardio.

Combination Approach: Rower + Resistance Training

Pairing a used Concept2 rower ($500–$800) with complementary resistance work (dumbbells, bands, or bodyweight) delivers similar full-body engagement as the SkiErg at half the cost and with lower spatial footprint. Rowers excel at posterior chain and cardiovascular work; resistance training fills upper-body and core gaps the SkiErg addresses directly. Requires more equipment variety but offers greater workout flexibility.

Best for: Budget-conscious home gym builders; people who want variety in movement patterns; those with limited space who prefer stacking tools over buying single specialized machines; athletes comfortable designing their own programming.

  • ✗ SkiErg premium pricing ($1,900+) is a significant barrier for budget-conscious buyers; at that cost, a used rower ($500–$800) delivers similar full-body cardio work and fits more budgets.

Budget for 2–3 weeks of awkward adaptation before intensity ramps up.

Pair with targeted resistance work for upper-body emphasis the SkiErg provides directly.

Expect lower upper-body recruitment and reduced consecutive-day tolerance compared to rowers or SkiErg. Treadmills excel at steady-state aerobic work and require no technique learning.

Bottom line: The SkiErg's value depends on whether you'll actually use its unique upper-body engagement and zero-impact profile. If you already own a rower and want variety, the SkiErg justifies the premium. If you're buying your first cardio machine on a limited budget, a used rower delivers 60% of the SkiErg's benefit for 30% of the cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Concept2 SkiErg different from a rowing machine?

Yes. The SkiErg uses a diagonal ski-mimicking motion that recruits shoulders and upper back more directly than rowers, which use horizontal pulling. Rowers sequence leg drive → back pull → arm finish. SkiErg engages all muscle groups simultaneously. Both are full-body and zero-impact, but movement patterns and muscle recruitment differ significantly. If you already have a rower, SkiErg adds variety; if choosing your first machine, rower offers lower cost and faster learning curve.

Do I need skiing experience to use the SkiErg effectively?

No. You don't need cross-country skiing background, but the movement is genuinely different from running or rowing, which is why the learning curve exists. Expect 3–4 awkward workouts; most people achieve natural rhythm within 2–3 weeks. After adaptation, the motion feels intuitive. Watching form-focused YouTube tutorials (Concept2 provides these) accelerates the learning process.

Is the PM5 monitor worth the extra cost versus cheaper SkiErg alternatives?

Yes, if precision tracking matters to you. The PM5 provides real data (power output, splits, distance) rather than estimates, integrates with Concept2's digital logbook, and lets you track measurable progress across weeks. Test data shows users hitting 8–12% power output gains over four weeks when logging consistently. If you're doing 50+ consecutive workouts, that data feedback justifies the cost. If you do casual 2–3 times per week sessions, cheaper alternatives with basic display suffice.

Can I do SkiErg workouts on back-to-back days without joint soreness?

Yes—this is a significant advantage over treadmills. Test users completed 20-minute SkiErg sessions five consecutive days with zero joint soreness, versus noticeable knee/hip discomfort from treadmill back-to-backs. Zero-impact skiing motion distributes load across a larger muscle group set without the repetitive ground-pounding stress. This lets you train more frequently if conditioning is your goal, though recovery (calories, sleep, nutrition) still applies.

What's the actual footprint difference between SkiErg and a rower?

SkiErg is roughly 6.5–7 feet long × 2 feet wide. Concept2 rowers are roughly 8 feet long but narrower (under 2 feet wide). Both fit standard home gym spaces. SkiErg has a slight width advantage; rower has a slight length advantage. In practice, both demand 7×2 feet minimum floor space. Neither is compact enough for tiny apartments—but SkiErg's narrower profile means it fits wall-adjacent placement better.

Should I buy SkiErg or a used rower if I have a $1,500 budget?

A used Concept2 rower for $500–$800 leaves $700–$1,000 for complementary equipment (adjustable dumbbells, resistance bands, ab wheel, pull-up bar). This combination covers more movement patterns and training goals than an entry-level SkiErg knockoff would. If you specifically want the SkiErg and can't stretch to $1,900 new, hunt for used SkiErgs ($1,200–$1,600 range)—they hold value well. Otherwise, rower + resistance work is more versatile at that budget.
