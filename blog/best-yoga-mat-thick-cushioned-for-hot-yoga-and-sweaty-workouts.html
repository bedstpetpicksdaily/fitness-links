What to Look For

  • Thickness of at least 6mm: Look for mats in the 6-8mm range. This provides meaningful cushioning for your joints—especially knees, hips, and wrists—without creating so much instability that balance poses become difficult. Anything thinner compromises joint protection in hot yoga, while anything thicker can interfere with your stability work.
  • Superior moisture and sweat resistance: Hot yoga and sweaty workouts demand non-porous, non-absorbent materials. Avoid mats that soak up sweat, as they become slippery, develop odor, and harbor bacteria. Look for closed-cell construction or rubber/PVC materials specifically designed to repel moisture while maintaining grip.
  • High-friction surface that works when wet: Your mat needs exceptional grip even when covered in sweat. Test reviews mention how the mat performs mid-practice when your hands and feet are wet. Premium TPE or natural rubber surfaces maintain traction better than lower-grade PVC materials as moisture accumulates.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What thickness is best for hot yoga mats?

    For hot yoga, a thickness of 4-5mm is ideal as it provides adequate cushioning without being too spongy, which can make balancing poses difficult. Thicker mats (6-8mm) offer more comfort for your joints but may compromise stability during standing poses where grip and responsiveness matter most.

    Do thick yoga mats get slippery when wet with sweat?

    A thick mat's slip resistance depends more on the material's surface texture than its thickness. Look for mats with closed-cell construction or textured surfaces specifically designed for hot yoga, as these provide better grip even when saturated with sweat compared to standard PVC or rubber mats.

    How do I clean a thick cushioned yoga mat after sweaty workouts?

    Wipe your mat immediately after use with a damp cloth or yoga mat spray to prevent sweat from soaking in. For deeper cleaning, hand wash with mild soap and water, then air dry completely (24 hours) to prevent mold and bacteria growth in the dense cushioning material.

    Will a thick yoga mat stay in place during hot yoga flow?

    Thick mats can shift more easily than thin ones, so choose one with a textured, non-slip surface and consider using a yoga towel on top for extra grip. A mat with quality rubber or PVC base will have better traction than softer materials, keeping it stable during transitions and sweaty flows.
