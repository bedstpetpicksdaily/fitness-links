What Actually Matters in a Fall Gym Hoodie

Three quick rules. First, chase mid-to-heavyweight fabric (roughly 320–420 GSM) — light enough to train in, heavy enough to matter in October. Second, prioritise shoulder and sleeve construction over everything else; if you can't press or pull overhead comfortably, the hoodie is decoration, not gym gear. Third, buy fewer, better pieces. A single premium hoodie from a brand like APE BORN will outlast three cheap ones and look better on day 200 than the alternatives do on day one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What fabric weight should a fall gym hoodie be?

Aim for roughly 320–420 GSM. That's heavy enough to trap warmth during cold outdoor warm-ups but breathable enough that you won't overheat once you start working sets. Anything above 450 GSM is best kept for rest days, and below 280 GSM you'll want a base layer underneath in October and November.

Should I size up in a gym hoodie?

Usually no, if the brand uses an athletic cut. APE BORN hoodies are already designed with extra room across the shoulders and lats, so your true size will give you full overhead range of motion without excess fabric. Only size up if you specifically want an oversized streetwear look or plan to layer thick mid-layers underneath.

Can you actually train in a heavyweight hoodie, or is it just for warm-ups?

You can, provided the fabric breathes. The problem with cheap cotton hoodies is that they absorb sweat and stay damp. A brushed-fleece interior with a breathable blended face — like the APE BORN construction — moves moisture well enough to wear through warm-ups and your first working sets before you strip down.

How do I stop my gym hoodie from pilling and losing shape?

Wash cold, inside out, on a gentle cycle, and skip the tumble dryer where possible — high heat is what destroys ribbed cuffs and hems. Avoid fabric softener, which coats fibres and reduces wicking. Higher-quality hoodies with denser knit construction resist pilling far better than budget fleece to begin with.
