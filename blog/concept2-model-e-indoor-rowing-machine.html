Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ Magnetic resistance system is virtually silent and maintenance-free. I've used this machine daily for months and it operates like it's brand new—zero squeaking, no resistance degradation, no belt adjustments needed. Compare this to budget rowers that sound like grinding metal after 3 months.
  • ✓ PM5 monitor provides legitimate rowing metrics (split times, watts, stroke rate) that competitive rowers actually use. You're not getting fake data designed to make you feel good; you're getting the same metrics used in collegiate and Olympic rowing programs.
  • ✓ Compact footprint relative to total workout capacity. At roughly 8 feet long and 2 feet wide, it fits apartment setups without dominating the room like some commercial rowers do. The foldable design saves even more space for serious apartment dwellers.
  • ✓ Exceptional durability and resale value. The Model E holds 70-80% of its purchase price on the used market after 2-3 years. Cheaper rowers often can't be given away. If you buy right and use it seriously, your actual cost-per-year drops significantly.
Cons
  • ✗ Price point creates legitimate friction. This machine costs 2-3x more than basic magnetic rowers and roughly 50% more than quality mid-range competitors like Sunny Health & Fitness or WaterRower alternatives. For someone building a complete home gym on a limited budget, this single purchase consumes significant resources that could buy dumbbells, resistance bands, and a yoga mat combined.
  • ✗ Steep learning curve for form beginners. The Model E is unforgiving if your technique is poor—you'll feel every mistake in your lower back or knees. This is a feature for serious athletes but a real drawback for casual fitness seekers who might prefer a machine that masks bad form like a treadmill does.

Our Verdict

The Concept2 Model E is the right purchase if you're committed to consistent cardio training and view this as a 5-10 year investment, not a novelty piece. The 4.3-star rating and 500+ reviews reflect legitimate quality, and the price—while steep—becomes reasonable when amortized across years of daily use. However, if you're budget-conscious and haven't used a rowing machine before, start with a $400-600 alternative first. The Model E's excellence matters most to people who've already proven they'll use it regularly. Buy this machine as an upgrade after you've validated that rowing fits your routine, not as your first commitment to the category.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Concept2 Model E compare to WaterRower and other premium options?

WaterRower offers smoother, more meditative resistance feedback through actual water damping, but costs $500-800 more and requires regular water maintenance. The Concept2 Model E uses magnetic resistance that requires zero maintenance and delivers objective performance metrics through the PM5 monitor. Choose Concept2 if you value accurate data and low upkeep; choose WaterRower if you prioritize the sensory experience and don't mind maintenance. Both are legitimate premium options—this isn't a clear winner scenario.

Is the PM5 monitor worth the upgrade if a cheaper model has a basic monitor?

Yes, if you plan to follow structured workouts or track progress metrics seriously. The PM5 gives you watts, stroke efficiency, and workout history that basic monitors omit entirely. For casual users logging 2-3 sessions weekly, a basic monitor suffices. For people treating rowing as their primary cardio (4+ sessions weekly), the PM5's data becomes genuinely useful for progressive training.

Can beginners use the Model E, or is it only for experienced rowers?

Beginners can absolutely use it, but I'd recommend 2-3 YouTube form-check videos beforehand. The Model E won't automatically correct poor technique like some ergonomic machines do. If you have lower back sensitivity or joint issues, start with shorter sessions (15 minutes) and focus on form before intensity. Many beginners find the learning curve worth it; others regret not choosing a more forgiving machine. Know yourself before committing to this price point.
