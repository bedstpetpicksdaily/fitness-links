Bowflex SelectTech 1090i Dumbbells: Worth It in 2026?

⏱8 min read

The Bowflex SelectTech 1090i has been a fixture in home gyms for nearly a decade, and for good reason—these adjustable dumbbells replace an entire rack with a single pair. But here's the thing: adjustable dumbbells have gotten crowded. Bowflex isn't the only player anymore, and the price premium has only climbed. We're going to cut through the marketing and tell you whether these actually deserve shelf space in your gym, or if you're just paying for the brand name.

July is peak buying season for home gym upgrades, and dumbbells are always first on the list. The SelectTech 1090i sits at a premium price point with 500+ customer reviews averaging 4.3 stars on Amazon. That's respectable but not flawless. Let's dig into what that rating actually means and whether these justify the investment compared to the flood of alternatives now available.

Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ The dial-and-click mechanism is genuinely fast—switching between weights takes 2-3 seconds once you get the motion down, which beats manual plate loading by miles and keeps workout momentum alive during supersets and circuit training.
  • ✓ The app integration (via Bluetooth) actually works without constant connectivity issues. You get rep tracking, workout history, and form cues that most competitors either skip or bundle poorly, and it syncs reliably enough that you won't spend 30 seconds reconnecting mid-set.
  • ✓ Space efficiency is real. The 1090i pair replaces roughly 32 dumbbells in a traditional setup—that's floor space savings that compound if you're working with a bedroom corner or basement nook rather than a full garage.
  • ✓ Build quality feels premium. The handle ergonomics are thoughtfully designed with a textured grip, and the dial mechanism hasn't shown reported wear after thousands of cycles in user reviews, which is notable in a category where cheap adjustables feel like they'll break by month six.
Cons
  • ✗ The price is genuinely steep. These run significantly higher than comparable fixed dumbbells for the same total poundage, and newer competitors like PowerBlocks and even Amazon-branded adjustables now offer 80% of the functionality for 40-50% less cost—which matters when you're spending four figures.
  • ✗ Weight increments stop at 90 lbs per dumbbell. If you're a serious lifter doing heavy upper body work, you'll plateau and won't be able to progress on movements like floor presses or single-arm rows—this is a hard ceiling for intermediate-to-advanced users, not a limitation you face with a traditional rack.

Our Verdict

The SelectTech 1090i earns its 4.3-star rating because it delivers on what it promises: fast weight changes, space savings, app tracking, and durable construction. But "delivers on promises" isn't the same as "worth the money in 2026." If you have under 300 square feet of gym space and training intensity that stays below 90 lbs per hand, these make sense. The app features add genuine value that cheaper adjustables skip. However, if you have the space for a dumbbell rack and you're lifting seriously, you'll outgrow these and feel like you overpaid. For casual home gym users prioritizing convenience over progression, they're a solid choice. For anyone else, compare pricing against PowerBlock Pro Series or even the Bowflex SelectTech 560i (the lighter sibling)—you might find better value aligned with your actual needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do the 1090i compare to the older SelectTech 1090 model?

The 'i' adds Bluetooth connectivity and the app experience—that's the primary difference. The dial mechanism, weight range (5-90 lbs), and handle design stayed the same. If you don't care about app tracking, the older 1090 can be found used for 30-40% less. The app is convenient but not revolutionary—don't pay the upgrade tax if tracking reps on your phone feels unnecessary.

Will these work for heavy compound lifts like deadlifts or bench press?

Yes and no. Single-arm work maxes out at 90 lbs, which is solid for isolation exercises but limiting for heavy bilateral movements. For bench press, goblet squats, or overhead work, you're looking at less total load than a barbell setup. The real sweet spot is accessory work, conditioning, and unilateral training where the 90 lb ceiling rarely matters.

How is the app actually useful, or is it just a gimmick?

The app logs sets, reps, and weight automatically if you stay connected (no manual entry), which saves time tracking. It also provides form feedback on certain exercises using your phone's camera. It's useful for people who want structured workout logging without thinking about it—less useful if you're already tracking workouts elsewhere or prefer dumbbell simplicity. Not a gimmick, but not essential either.

Should I buy these new or look for used pairs online?

Significant price drops happen on used pairs—expect 25-35% savings if you buy secondhand. The mechanism is durable enough that used pairs typically function fine, and Bowflex's warranty transfers on most platforms. If budget is tight, used is smart. If you want the confidence of new equipment and warranty coverage, the new price is painful but justified for longevity.

How do these compare to PowerBlock Pro Series or adjustables from other brands?

PowerBlock is lighter and more portable, making them better for travel-adjacent home gyms. Bowflex offers better app integration and a slightly more refined dial. Newer budget brands have closed the gap on build quality while undercutting price by 40-50%. The honest answer: Bowflex is safer (proven track record), PowerBlock is better for portability, and budget alternatives are better for value. Pick based on what you prioritize, not brand loyalty.
