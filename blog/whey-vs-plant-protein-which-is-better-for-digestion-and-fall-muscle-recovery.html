By Best Fitness Picks Daily • September 03, 2026 • Contains affiliate links

Fall is when training loads quietly creep up. Marathon block, hockey season, back-to-the-gym motivation after a lazy August — whatever your version looks like, your protein intake matters more in October than it did in July. But the question most people actually wrestle with isn't how much protein, it's which kind: fast-digesting whey, or a plant blend that's gentler on the gut?

The honest answer is that both work for muscle repair, and the research on total daily protein intake is far more important than the source. Where they genuinely diverge is digestion, leucine density per scoop, cost per gram, and what else is packed into the tub. So instead of another vague "plant protein is cleaner" article, we put four real products side by side: Power Life Foundation Four (Tony Horton's plant-based 4-in-1 shake), Optimum Nutrition Gold Standard 100% Whey, Orgain Organic Protein, and Ritual Essential Protein Daily Shake 18+.

Each one wins on something. None of them wins on everything. Here's the breakdown, including where Power Life is genuinely worth the premium — and where it isn't.

Power Life Foundation Four — the "everything in one scoop" plant option

Power Life's Foundation Four is not a bare-bones protein powder, and that's the whole point of it. Each serving delivers roughly 20g of plant protein (a pea-based blend) alongside a digestive enzyme complex, prebiotic fiber, and MCT-type fats — the four "foundations" in the name. That combination is aimed squarely at the over-40 lifter who finds a big whey shake sits like a brick, and who also wants a little more from their morning drink than macros. Practically, this means you're not buying a separate enzyme supplement or fiber scoop, which changes the real-world math.

Cost is where you have to be clear-eyed. Optimum Nutrition Gold Standard 100% Whey — the price-per-gram benchmark

Nothing on this list beats ON Gold Standard on value or on raw muscle-building efficiency. A 5 lb tub typically runs $70–$85 for roughly 70+ servings, putting you near $1.00–$1.20 per 24g serving — often under 5 cents per gram of protein. It's whey isolate-forward, which means fast absorption and around 5.5g of leucine-rich BCAAs per scoop, the amino acid profile most directly tied to muscle protein synthesis. For a post-lift shake in a heavy fall training block, it's the most efficient tool here, full stop.

The catch is digestion. Gold Standard is not lactose-free, and while it does contain a small enzyme blend, a meaningful minority of users report bloating or gas — especially at 2+ scoops a day. It also uses artificial sweeteners in most flavors. If your gut handles dairy fine, this is the smart-money pick and the other three have to justify their premium. If dairy is the reason you're reading this article, no amount of value fixes that.

Orgain Organic Protein — the budget plant-based mainstream pick

Orgain is the plant protein most people meet first, usually at Costco or Target. Around $27–$32 for a 2.03 lb tub (20 servings) puts it near $1.30–$1.60 per serving for 21g of protein from a pea, brown rice, and chia blend. It's USDA Organic, vegan, and available basically everywhere, which is not a small advantage — you can restock on a Tuesday without waiting on shipping.

Where it falls short of Power Life: it's a protein powder with fiber, not a formulated digestive product. There's no meaningful enzyme complex, and the brown rice component gives it a chalkier, grittier texture than most drinkers love straight in water. Orgain has also historically drawn scrutiny in third-party heavy metal testing of plant proteins (a category-wide issue for rice- and pea-based powders, not unique to this brand). It's a solid, affordable, widely available plant option — just don't expect it to solve gut issues on its own.

Ritual Essential Protein Daily Shake 18+ — the transparency play

Ritual charges about $40 for 20 servings (~$2 per serving) for 20g of protein from a single source: organic pea protein grown and processed in the U.S., with traceable supply chain information down to the supplier. It's third-party tested, Informed Sport-style clean-label positioning, no added sugar, and sweetened with organic stevia. It also includes choline. For people who care most about knowing exactly where their protein came from, nobody here beats Ritual.

But it's minimalist by design. There's no enzyme blend, no MCTs, no prebiotic fiber package — so at essentially the same price as Power Life you're getting fewer functional ingredients. Some users also find single-source pea protein the hardest on their stomach of the plant options, since it lacks the complementary blend and enzymes that ease digestion. Ritual is the transparency winner; Power Life is the digestion-formulation winner at a similar price point.

The digestion question, honestly

Plant protein isn't inherently easier to digest — pea protein causes GI complaints for plenty of people too. What actually helps is (1) avoiding lactose if you're sensitive, (2) added digestive enzymes like protease and bromelain, and (3) not slamming 50g at once. Power Life's advantage here is structural: enzymes are built in. Whey's advantage is absorption speed and leucine content. For fall recovery specifically, if you train hard and your gut tolerates dairy, whey still edges plant protein on per-scoop muscle signaling. If bloating is derailing your consistency, the best protein is the one you'll actually drink every day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is whey or plant protein better for building muscle in a fall training block?

Whey has a slight per-scoop edge because of its higher leucine content and faster absorption, which are tied to muscle protein synthesis. But studies comparing matched daily protein intakes show the gap largely disappears when total intake is equal. If plant protein means you actually drink your shake every day without stomach issues, it will outperform whey you skip.

Do digestive enzymes in a protein powder actually do anything?

For some people, yes. Protease and bromelain-type enzymes can help break down protein and reduce the bloating and gas that some users get from concentrated protein doses, particularly plant proteins high in fiber and oligosaccharides. They're not magic, and if your issue is specifically lactose, simply switching off whey does more than any enzyme blend.

Is Power Life Foundation Four worth double the price of Optimum Nutrition?

Only if you value the extras. On pure protein grams, Optimum Nutrition wins decisively. Power Life's case rests on being dairy-free and bundling enzymes, prebiotic fiber, and MCTs into one scoop — which is genuinely worth it if you'd otherwise buy those separately or if whey consistently upsets your stomach. Otherwise it's an expensive way to buy 20g of pea protein.
