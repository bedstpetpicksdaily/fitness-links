Rowing machines are notorious for becoming expensive closet fixtures. But the market has genuinely split into distinct categories—each serving different buyers with different priorities. The question isn't whether rowing is effective; it's which machine aligns with how you actually exercise, your space constraints, your budget, and your relationship with digital coaching and community.

Before committing to any of these three rowers, test the ergonomic fit in person if possible, since the Hydrow Wave's compact footprint and the Model D/E's different seat designs will feel significantly different during longer sessions, and an uncomfortable machine becomes an expensive closet decoration. Budget an additional 15-20% for delivery and assembly since all three are heavy, bulky items that rarely fit through standard doorways without professional setup assistance.

Editor's Tip

This guide compares the major options currently available: premium connected machines (Hydrow Wave), commercial-grade fluid-damper systems (Concept2 Model D), modern magnetic alternatives (Concept2 Model E), and sub-$600 budget models. Each represents a fundamentally different value proposition, and the "best" choice depends entirely on your situation.

Hydrow Wave (Standard)

A magnetic-resistance connected rower with a 22-inch touchscreen, AI coaching, and live/on-demand class library. Features 16-level resistance, whisper-quiet operation, a compact footprint suitable for apartments, and real-time form feedback from the AI system. The PM5 monitor equivalent tracks performance metrics and syncs with the Hydrow platform. Priced around $2,000, it sits in the premium-accessible tier with a 4.3-star rating across 500+ verified reviews.

Best for: Busy professionals and parents who need motivation baked into the machine; people with limited space; users who struggle with consistency and benefit from structured coaching and live class community.

Hydrow Wave Plus

The premium version of Hydrow's lineup, priced around $3,000+. It includes everything in the standard Wave—22-inch screen, AI coaching, magnetic resistance—plus enhanced features for serious rowers. Real users report that the AI Coach maintains accountability and engagement past the initial enthusiasm phase, with the combination of visual feedback, structured guidance, and social elements creating sustained usage patterns.

Best for: Serious rowers committed to daily or multi-weekly sessions; users who want premium build quality and advanced AI form correction; buyers willing to invest in the full connected fitness ecosystem.

Concept2 Model D

Concept2's premium flagship at $900–$1,200. Features a sealed chain drive and fluid damper system, measuring 96 inches long by 24 inches wide, folding vertically to roughly 14×24 inches. Includes the PM5 monitor with precision-grade accuracy (to the tenth of a second), syncing with Concept2's online logbook for multi-month progress tracking. Used in CrossFit boxes, university rowing programs, and competitive home gyms worldwide.

Best for: Competitive rowers, serious athletes, CrossFit users, and buyers prioritizing longevity and resale value. Users who want the proven durability standard and don't mind the maintenance requirements.

Concept2 Model E

Concept2's magnetic-resistance alternative to the Model D, featuring virtually silent operation with zero maintenance required. More compact (approximately 8 feet long, 2 feet wide) and apartment-friendly storage. Includes the same PM5 monitor with competitive rowing metrics as the Model D. Magnetic system requires no belt adjustments or resistance degradation management. Priced slightly lower than the Model D based on current market positioning.

Best for: Apartment dwellers and noise-sensitive users; buyers seeking maintenance-free operation; those wanting proven Concept2 durability without the fluid damper learning curve or ongoing upkeep.

Budget Magnetic Rowers (Sub-$600)

Entry-level magnetic rowers with basic monitors and magnetic resistance systems. These machines typically sound like grinding metal after 3 months, degrade in resistance quality over time, and hold little to no resale value. They represent the bare minimum for at-home rowing and are frequently abandoned within the first few months of ownership.

Best for: Users on extremely tight budgets who view this as a disposable experiment; buyers who genuinely cannot afford $800+ and are willing to accept poor durability and degraded experience.

Our Verdict

For committed home fitness enthusiasts with limited space and a desire for structured coaching: The Hydrow Wave (Standard, $2,000) wins. The 22-inch screen, AI form feedback, and live class community create behavioral momentum that keeps machines from becoming clothes racks. The compact footprint matters in apartments. The real-world 4.3-star rating reflects sustained usage, not just initial enthusiasm.

For competitive rowers, CrossFit athletes, and users prioritizing resale value: The Concept2 Model D ($900–$1,200) remains the gold standard. Its fluid damper and sealed chain drive are industry standard. The PM5 monitor's precision tracking and online logbook integration serve serious training. Durability is unmatched; these machines hold 60–70% resale value after 3+ years. You sacrifice space and must accept maintenance, but you gain credibility and longevity.

For apartment dwellers and noise-sensitive users who want proven durability without maintenance: The Concept2 Model E represents genuine value. You get Concept2 reliability and the PM5 monitor at a lower price point, plus silent operation and zero damper upkeep. Trade-off: the magnetic system doesn't feel identical to fluid damping for serious competitive training, but for fitness rowing, it's imperceptible.

For budget-constrained buyers: Skip sub-$600 budget models entirely. They degrade within months, sound terrible, and create false economy—you'll either repurchase or abandon fitness rowing. If cost is critical, buy a used Concept2 Model D ($600–$800 on secondary markets) or save for a new Model E instead.

The real decision tree: (1) Do you value structured coaching and community engagement? Choose Hydrow Wave. (2) Do you plan competitive training or need maximum resale value? Choose Concept2 Model D. (3) Do you need silent operation and zero maintenance in a smaller footprint? Choose Concept2 Model E. (4) Is your budget under $600? Accept that you're experimenting, not investing—or wait and buy used.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the main difference between Concept2 Model D and Model E?

Damper system. Model D uses fluid damping (feels like pushing water, requires maintenance, louder, 96 inches). Model E uses magnetic resistance (virtually silent, zero maintenance, more compact, ~8 feet). Both have the same PM5 monitor and precision tracking. Model D dominates competitive rowing; Model E wins for apartments and low-maintenance users.

Is Hydrow Wave worth $2,000 over a used Concept2 Model D at $600–$800?

Depends on your behavior. Hydrow's 22-inch screen, AI coaching, and live classes create engagement that keeps people rowing. Concept2 is silent, proven, and requires no subscription. If you struggle with consistency and need structured motivation, Hydrow's behavioral advantage justifies the cost. If you're self-motivated, the Concept2 is better value.

Why shouldn't I buy a $300–$600 budget rower?

They degrade rapidly (grinding sounds within months), lose resistance quality, have poor build tolerances, and hold zero resale value. A $300 machine often becomes a $300 loss within 6 months. Either invest in a real machine or buy used. The financial logic breaks down—you're not saving; you're delaying a real purchase.

Do I need the Hydrow Wave Plus ($3,000+) or is the standard Wave ($2,000) enough?

The standard Wave is sufficient for most users. The Plus adds enhanced AI capabilities and premium build quality but requires daily/multi-weekly commitment to justify the $1,000+ premium. Start with the standard Wave unless you're already an established rower with a consistent routine.

Can I use Concept2 without the PM5 monitor?

Yes, but the PM5 is worth the investment. It provides precision tracking, splits to the tenth of a second, and syncs with Concept2's online logbook for progress monitoring. Without it, you lose data-driven training and motivation. Factor the PM5 into your total Concept2 cost.

What's the real cost of Hydrow Wave ownership?

Initial machine: ~$2,000. Monthly subscription for classes/AI coaching: ~$30–$40/month ($360–$480 annually). After 3 years, total cost of ownership approaches $3,000+. Concept2 Model D costs $900–$1,200 upfront with minimal ongoing costs. If you'll use it for 5+ years, Hydrow's cost-per-use becomes competitive; if you quit after 18 months, it's expensive.
