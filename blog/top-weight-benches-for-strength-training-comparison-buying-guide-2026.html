Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between adjustable and flat benches?

Adjustable benches allow you to change the backrest angle for incline, flat, and decline positions, providing exercise variety. Flat benches remain stationary and are preferred for heavy compound lifts due to maximum stability. Most lifters benefit from adjustable benches for home gyms, while serious powerlifters often prefer flat benches for pure strength work.

How much weight capacity do I need?

Your bench's weight capacity should exceed your body weight plus the maximum weight you plan to lift by at least 25%. If you weigh 200 lbs and plan to bench press 300 lbs, choose a bench with at least 750 lb capacity. This safety margin ensures stability and longevity.

Are FED Fitness benches worth the investment?

Yes. FED Fitness benches feature commercial-grade construction, premium materials, and excellent customer support. While they may cost more upfront than budget brands, their durability and stability make them cost-effective long-term investments that won't need replacing in 5-10 years.

What's the ideal bench padding thickness?

Look for benches with 2-3 inches of high-density foam padding. This provides adequate comfort while maintaining stability during heavy lifts. Too much padding compromises force transfer, while too little causes discomfort during extended sessions.

Can I use a weight bench for other exercises?

Absolutely. Quality benches like those from FED Fitness support dumbbell exercises, step-ups, Bulgarian split squats, and more. Adjustable benches expand possibilities further, allowing incline dumbbell presses, decline sit-ups, and various isolation movements.
