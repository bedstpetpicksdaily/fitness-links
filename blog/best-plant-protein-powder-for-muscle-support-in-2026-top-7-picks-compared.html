This comparison puts Power Life Foundation Four, the plant-based shake from Tony Horton's supplement line, up against six competitors people actually buy: Vega Sport Premium Protein, Garden of Life Sport Organic Plant-Based, Orgain Organic Protein, Ritual Essential Protein Daily Shake 18+, KOS Organic Plant Protein and Naked Pea. We looked at grams of protein per scoop, leucine content (the amino acid that actually triggers muscle protein synthesis), added digestive enzymes, third-party testing, cost per serving and how each one mixes and tastes.

Spoiler: there is no single winner. The right pick depends on whether you're chasing maximum grams per dollar, a full meal-replacement shake, or a certified-clean powder for competition. Formulas and prices change, so always check the current label before you buy — but here's how the seven stack up as of early 2026.

Power Life Foundation Four

Foundation Four is Power Life's flagship plant shake, and it's positioned less as a bare protein powder and more as an all-in-one daily foundation — hence the name. Each serving delivers roughly 20g of plant protein from a pea-and-rice base (a combination that covers the methionine gap pea protein alone has), plus added vitamins and minerals, a prebiotic/fiber component and digestive support ingredients. That's the pitch: instead of buying protein, a greens powder and a probiotic separately, you buy one canister. Pricing typically lands in the $50–$60 range per tub, with a meaningful discount for subscription, which puts it around $2–$3 per serving depending on how you order. That's premium territory. What you're paying for is the bundle and the formulation-for-over-40s angle — Power Life's whole line is built around active adults in their 40s, 50s and 60s who care as much about digestion and joint comfort as about hypertrophy. If you already take a multivitamin and a fiber supplement, you're double-paying. If you don't, the math gets a lot more favorable. You can check current pricing and the full ingredient panel at mypowerlife.com.

Vega Sport Premium Protein

Vega Sport is the serious-training benchmark in this category and the most direct performance rival to anything on this list. It packs about 30g of protein per serving from pea, pumpkin seed, sunflower seed and alfalfa, along with roughly 5g of BCAAs and added tart cherry and probiotics for recovery. Critically, it's NSF Certified for Sport — the certification that matters if you're drug-tested for competition, which Power Life does not carry. Expect around $45–$55 for a 20-serving tub, or roughly $2.25–$2.75 per serving. Downsides: the texture is grittier than Orgain or Power Life, the flavor is polarizing (the chocolate is widely liked, the vanilla less so), and there's no meaningful vitamin/mineral profile — it's a protein powder, full stop. If your only goal is maximum protein and leucine per scoop with tested purity, Vega Sport beats Foundation Four on paper.

Garden of Life Sport Organic Plant-Based Protein

Garden of Life Sport is the other NSF Certified for Sport option, and it's the most certification-heavy product here: USDA Organic, Non-GMO Project Verified, and Informed Choice in some SKUs. You get about 30g of protein per scoop from a pea, navy bean, lentil, garbanzo and cranberry seed blend, plus tart cherry, turmeric and a probiotic/enzyme complex. Price sits around $45–$50 for 19–20 servings. Compared with Power Life, Garden of Life gives you 10 more grams of protein and organic certification, but the recovery blend is thinner on actual micronutrient coverage and the mouthfeel is noticeably chalkier. This is the pick for someone who wants organic and competition-legal at the same time and doesn't mind an earthy taste.

Orgain Organic Protein Powder

Orgain is the value king of mainstream plant protein and the one you'll find in every Costco and Target. Around 21g of protein per serving from pea, brown rice and chia, USDA Organic, at roughly $25–$32 for a 20-serving 2.03 lb tub — about $1.30 per serving, or less than half what Power Life costs. It mixes well in water, tastes sweet (some find it too sweet), and is genuinely good enough for general muscle support. Where it loses to Foundation Four: no meaningful vitamin/mineral fortification in the standard version, a lighter digestive enzyme story, and Orgain has faced consumer scrutiny over heavy-metal testing in plant proteins generally — worth noting the brand publishes testing info but isn't NSF Certified for Sport. If budget is the deciding factor, Orgain is hard to argue with.

Ritual Essential Protein Daily Shake 18+

Ritual takes the opposite approach to Power Life: radical minimalism. About 20g of protein from a single source — organic pea protein grown in the U.S. and Canada — plus added L-methionine and L-leucine to complete the amino profile, and nothing else beyond stevia and flavoring. Every ingredient is traceable to its supplier on their site, and it's Informed Sport Certified. Price is roughly $40 for 15–16 servings, or about $2.50 per serving, which is expensive for 20g and no extras. Ritual wins on transparency and clean-label credibility; Power Life wins on value-per-scoop if you actually want the added vitamins, fiber and enzymes. Ritual's vanilla is one of the better-tasting stevia-sweetened plant proteins, though it's thin in water.

KOS Organic Plant Protein

KOS sits between Orgain and Power Life. Roughly 20g of protein per serving from pea, flax, quinoa, pumpkin seed and chia, with a five-enzyme digestive blend (DigeSEB) and a modest vitamin/mineral addition — a formula philosophy quite similar to Foundation Four, at a lower price. Around $40 for 28 servings works out to roughly $1.40–$1.50 per serving. KOS's Chocolate is arguably the best-tasting plant protein in this entire lineup, thanks to real cocoa and coconut milk powder, which also makes it creamier than most. It's not NSF certified and the micronutrient doses are modest, but on a straight features-per-dollar basis, KOS undercuts Power Life meaningfully.

Naked Pea

Naked Pea is the anti-formula option: one ingredient, yellow pea protein from U.S. and Canadian farms, nothing added. About 27g of protein and 5.7g of BCAAs per serving, third-party tested for heavy metals, at roughly $50–$60 for a 5 lb tub with 76 servings — which lands near $0.70 per serving, by far the cheapest here. Unflavored means you can put it in anything, but drinking it plain in water is genuinely unpleasant; most people blend it with fruit, oats or cocoa. There are no enzymes, no vitamins, no probiotics, no flavor masking. If you're a high-volume user optimizing purely for grams of protein per dollar and you already own a blender, Naked Pea is the rational choice and nothing on this list comes close on price.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 20g of plant protein enough for muscle growth?

It can be, but it's borderline. Research suggests roughly 2.5–3g of leucine per serving is needed to maximally trigger muscle protein synthesis, and plant proteins are lower in leucine than whey. A 20g pea/rice serving typically lands near that threshold, while 30g servings clear it comfortably. If you're using a 20g product like Power Life Foundation Four, Ritual or Orgain for post-training recovery, consider using 1.5 scoops or pairing it with a leucine-rich whole food.

Does Power Life Foundation Four replace a meal?

It's positioned closer to a meal-support shake than a bare protein powder because of the added vitamins, minerals and fiber, but calorie content per serving is still modest compared with a full meal-replacement product. Most people use it as a breakfast base with a banana and nut butter, or as a post-workout shake. Check the current nutrition panel, as formulas are periodically updated.

Which of these plant proteins tastes best?

In our comparison, KOS Chocolate was the clear flavor and texture winner thanks to real cocoa and coconut milk powder, with Orgain a close second for sweetness-forward palates. Power Life Foundation Four mixes smoothly and avoids the chalkiness of Garden of Life Sport and Vega Sport. Naked Pea is unflavored and needs to be blended with fruit or cocoa to be drinkable.

Do I need an NSF Certified for Sport protein?

Only if you compete in a drug-tested sport — NCAA, professional leagues, or tested federations — where a contaminated supplement can end a season. In that case, Vega Sport, Garden of Life Sport or Ritual (Informed Sport) are the safe picks. For general fitness, third-party heavy-metal testing matters more than sport certification, and most reputable brands here publish some testing information.
