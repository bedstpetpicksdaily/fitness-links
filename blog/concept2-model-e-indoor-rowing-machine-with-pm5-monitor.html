Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Concept2 Model E compare to other $1000+ rowing machines?

The Model E occupies a specific lane: it's the consumer-focused machine from Concept2, the company that dominates competitive rowing. The main difference from their pricier models (Model D, Dynamic) is that the E uses a chain drive instead of a belt, which means slightly more maintenance and a different feel. For home use, this trade-off is worth the $300-500 savings. Compared to brands like WaterRower or NordicTrack, the E prioritizes simplicity and durability over flashy tech—no on-demand classes, just pure rowing. If you want ecosystem integration, you might prefer those; if you want a machine that won't need repairs in five years, the E wins.

Is the PM5 monitor worth the upgrade over the PM4 or basic display?

Yes, genuinely. The PM5 connects to your phone, stores your workout data, and lets you compete on Concept2's online leaderboards if you care about that. More importantly, it tracks splits and pace with precision that matters for progression. The PM4 is simpler and cheaper, but the $100-150 difference for the PM5 buys you data visibility that keeps people engaged long-term. I've seen people stick with machines because they could actually see their improvements; vague feedback leads to people quitting.

Can I actually use this in an apartment without disturbing neighbors?

The Model E is quieter than most cardiovascular equipment, but it's not silent. The chain and flywheel create a mechanical hum that's noticeable, especially in quiet buildings. Putting it on a rubber mat helps absorb vibration. The real noise comes from you—the effort, the breathing—which is unavoidable. If your neighbors are extremely sensitive to noise and you're rowing at 6am, they might notice. For normal daytime use or evening workouts, it's respectful enough. If you absolutely need silent equipment, a rower isn't your best option regardless of brand.

How often does the Model E need maintenance?

Minimal, but consistent. Wipe down the chain and lubricate it every few months, keep the seat gliders clean, and check that bolts haven't loosened. Unlike motorized equipment, there's very little that can break. The damper system is sealed and maintenance-free. Most owners report zero issues over years. Compare this to treadmills or bikes that develop mechanical problems regularly, and the E looks like a maintenance dream. Budget maybe 30 minutes every six months if you're using it three-four times per week.

What's the realistic calorie burn compared to other home cardio equipment?

Rowing burns approximately 8-15 calories per minute depending on your effort level and body weight—this is comparable to running on a treadmill at moderate-to-hard intensity. The PM5 calculates this based on your power output, so the numbers are reasonably accurate (not inflated like some equipment). The advantage is that rowing engages 85% of your muscle mass, meaning you build strength while burning calories, which treadmills don't do. It's not inherently 'better' than other cardio, but it's more efficient if your goal is total-body development.
