Rebounder Sizes Explained: 36", 40", 44" and 50"

Mini trampolines are measured by their outer frame diameter, and the four sizes you'll encounter most often are 36-inch, 40-inch, 44-inch and 48–50-inch. A 36" model is the most portable and ideal for tight apartments, gentle health bouncing, or users under about 5'4" — but the smaller jump surface means less room for lateral moves and jogging drills. The 40" size is the sweet spot for most solo home users: enough surface area for a proper workout, still light enough to move and store upright behind a door. Move up to 44" and you gain meaningful freedom for jumping jacks, twists, high knees and choreographed bounce fitness routines — this is the size most studio classes use. The 48–50" models are best for taller users, heavier body weights, or anyone who wants a near-studio experience and has the floor space to commit.

Matching Rebounder Size to Your Space and Goals

Before you buy, measure your available floor area and add at least 30cm of clearance on every side, plus vertical headroom. A useful rule: your ceiling height should be at least your standing height plus 60cm, since even modest bounces lift you 15–25cm. If you're bouncing under a standard 2.4m ceiling and you're under 6ft, you'll be fine on any size. Also check whether the rebounder legs detach or fold — Boogie Bounce models with removable legs slide under a bed or sofa easily, which effectively makes a 44" rebounder viable in a room where you assumed only a 36" would fit.

Then think about goals. Gentle rebounding for circulation, bone density and recovery? A 36–40" is plenty. Fat-loss cardio, class-style choreography, or sharing with a partner? Go 44" or larger. Heavier users should also check the stated user weight limit — quality bungee rebounders typically support 100–150kg, and the higher-capacity frames tend to come in the larger diameters. Common Sizing Mistakes to Avoid

The biggest mistake is buying the smallest rebounder available purely on price and storage grounds, then finding the bounce feels stiff and cramped within a fortnight. The second is assuming bigger always means better — an oversized rebounder in a small room means you'll bounce timidly to avoid clipping furniture, which defeats the purpose. Finally, don't ignore the suspension system: two rebounders of identical diameter can feel completely different depending on whether they use steel springs or bungee cords. For home use, bungee wins on quietness, joint comfort and rebound quality almost every time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size rebounder is best for a small apartment?

A 36" or 40" rebounder is ideal for small spaces. Look for a model with removable or folding legs so it can be stored under a bed or upright behind a door, and choose bungee suspension rather than springs to keep noise down for neighbours.

Do I need a rebounder with a handlebar?

A handlebar isn't essential, but it's highly recommended if you're a beginner, over 60, recovering from injury, or plan to do fast-paced interval work. Boogie Bounce offers adjustable T-bar options that can be removed once your balance improves.

How much ceiling height do I need for rebounding?

As a guide, allow your standing height plus around 60cm. Most rebounding lifts you 15–25cm off the mat, so a standard 2.4m ceiling works for the majority of users. Also leave at least 30cm of clearance around the frame.
