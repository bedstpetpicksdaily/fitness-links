    Frequently Asked Questions

    What resistance band weight should I choose for home workouts?

    Choose a resistance level based on your fitness level: light (10-20 lbs) for beginners, medium (30-50 lbs) for intermediate, and heavy (60+ lbs) for advanced users. Heavy-duty bands typically range from 50-200 lbs of resistance, so consider starting with a set that includes multiple resistance levels to progress over time.

    Are heavy-duty resistance bands better than weights for home workouts?

    Heavy-duty resistance bands offer unique advantages: they're space-efficient, portable, create constant tension throughout movements, and are gentler on joints than free weights. However, they work best when combined with dumbbells or used for isolation exercises, as they're less ideal for heavy compound lifts like squats and deadlifts.

    How long do heavy-duty resistance bands last before breaking?

    Quality heavy-duty resistance bands last 1-3 years with proper care and regular use, though some premium brands claim longevity up to 5 years. Durability depends on material quality, frequency of use, proper storage away from direct sunlight, and avoiding sharp edges—investing in latex or fabric-coated bands significantly extends lifespan.

    What exercises can you do with heavy-duty resistance bands at home?

    Heavy-duty bands work for chest presses, rows, shoulder presses, lateral raises, bicep curls, tricep extensions, squats, deadlifts, and leg abductions. They're particularly effective for activation exercises, functional training, and adding variable resistance to bodyweight movements—making them versatile for full-body home workouts.
