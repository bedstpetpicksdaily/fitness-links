What to Look For

1. Cork Quality and Thickness

  • Look for 100% natural cork (not cork dust mixed with plastic binders)
  • Minimum 5mm thickness for joint protection and durability
  • Dense, uniform cork surface without crumbly edges
  • Certifications like FSC (Forest Stewardship Council) for sustainable sourcing

2. Alignment Line Quality and Visibility

  • Lines should be printed on the surface, not just silk-screened (they last longer)
  • High contrast colors (white, grey, or neon) for visibility during practice
  • Lines positioned for standard poses: downward dog, warrior poses, and plank alignment
  • Check if lines cover the entire mat or just key zones

3. Base Layer and Grip Technology

  • Non-slip rubber or TPE backing prevents sliding on hardwood floors
  • Natural latex backing aligns with eco-friendly values
  • Test grip reviews specifically for wet conditions (cork excels here)
  • Mat should not curl at edges after extended use

4. Dimensions, Weight, and Portability

  • Standard 68" x 24" fits most bodies; longer mats available for taller practitioners
  • Cork mats weigh 3-5 lbs (heavier than PVC but lighter than you'd expect)
  • Consider carrying straps and whether you'll take it to studios or use it at home
  • Check water and sweat resistance (cork handles moisture better than traditional mats)
