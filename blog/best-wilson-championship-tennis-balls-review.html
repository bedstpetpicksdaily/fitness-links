What to Look For When Buying Tennis Balls

Pressurized vs. Pressureless Construction

Wilson Championship balls come pressurized, which means they have a longer initial bounce and better performance right out of the can. Pressurized balls are ideal if you play regularly and want consistent performance for competitive matches. They do lose pressure over time (typically playable for 4-6 weeks with regular use), but the superior feel during peak performance makes them worth it for serious players.

Felt Material & Durability Rating

Look for high-quality felt that resists pilling and fuzz buildup. Wilson's felt is designed to hold up to hard court abuse, which is crucial if you play on concrete or clay surfaces multiple times weekly. The thicker felt construction means less frequent replacements—a key factor when comparing long-term cost per use.

Can Seal & Freshness Guarantee

Check that the pressurized can has an airtight seal and recent manufacturing date. Tennis balls naturally depressurize over time, so older stock means you're paying for balls that won't perform at peak levels. When ordering online, prioritize listings that explicitly state "recently manufactured" or show current batch codes.

Court Type Compatibility

Wilson Championship balls perform identically across all court surfaces (hard court, clay, grass), but their regular-duty felt works best on standard hard courts. If you primarily play on clay, consider their clay-court specific line. For most recreational and competitive players, the standard Championship balls are the universal choice.

How long do Wilson Championship tennis balls last?

Wilson Championship balls typically last 4-6 weeks with regular recreational play (3-4 times per week), though this varies based on court surface and playing intensity. What's the difference between Wilson Championship and Wilson US Open balls?

Do Wilson Championship tennis balls work on all court surfaces?
