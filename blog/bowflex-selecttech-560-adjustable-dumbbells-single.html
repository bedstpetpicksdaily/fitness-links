Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ Dial-based weight selection is genuinely fast and intuitive. You twist to your target weight, grab, and go. Unlike pin-stack systems, there's zero fumbling between sets, which matters when you're maintaining momentum in a workout.
  • ✓ Compact footprint saves serious space. The SelectTech 560 occupies roughly the same floor area as a single fixed dumbbell despite holding 5 to 25 pounds. For apartment dwellers and small home gyms, this is a legitimate game-changer.
  • ✓ Build quality feels premium without the premium price of boutique brands. The metal frame and rubberized handle hold up to heavy use, and owners report minimal wear after years of regular training.
  • ✓ Weight increments jump by 2.5 pounds between steps, which is granular enough for progressive overload without excessive jumps. You can actually dial in the right weight instead of compromising with what's available.
Cons
  • ✗ The 25-pound ceiling is restrictive for serious strength athletes. If you're past the beginner stage in pressing or rowing movements, you'll outgrow this dumbbell quickly. Advanced lifters need SelectTech 1090s or better—this feels like training wheels in comparison.
  • ✗ The dial mechanism accumulates dust and debris over time, particularly in humid home gyms. Occasional cleaning is necessary to keep adjustments smooth, and some owners report stiffness after 12+ months of neglect.

Our Verdict

The Bowflex SelectTech 560 justifies its price for intermediate lifters in space-constrained homes, especially those mixing strength work with functional fitness or rehabilitation training. It's not the cheapest adjustable dumbbell, but it's also not premium-priced—and the speed of adjustment matters more than people expect when you're actually working out. Grab it if you're building out a home gym from scratch or upgrading from fixed weights and you're realistic about the weight ceiling. Skip it if you're already strong enough to bench press 25 pounds for high reps or if you need something that doubles as a primary strength tool. For most intermediate home gym users, it's a solid tactical choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the SelectTech 560 better than fixed dumbbells for a beginner?

Yes, but with a caveat. A single adjustable dumbbell replaces 5 to 10 fixed dumbbells, so you save money and space—both huge advantages for someone starting out. The trade-off is you can only use one weight at a time, which limits supersets and paired exercises. For pure convenience and progression, the 560 wins. For variety, fixed pairs still have merit.

Can you use two SelectTech 560s for bilateral movements like bench press?

Technically yes, but it's expensive and impractical. You'd need to buy two units, which puts you in the price range of better adjustable dumbbell pairs. SelectTech 1090s (which go up to 90 pounds) come as pairs and are a smarter investment if bilateral training is your priority.

How does the 4.3-star rating compare to similar products?

It's solid middle ground. Premium brands like PowerBlocks score slightly higher (4.5+), but they cost 40-60% more. Budget adjustable dumbbells dip to 3.8-4.0 stars due to durability issues. The 500+ reviews behind the 4.3 rating mean it's based on real volume, not a small sample size. That consistency matters more than chasing a fractionally higher score.

Does the SelectTech 560 work well for rehabilitation or physical therapy?

Absolutely. This is one of the SelectTech's strongest use cases. The smooth weight increments and dial mechanism let you start at 5 pounds and progress methodically, which rehab protocols demand. Hand strength and grip fatigue aren't concerns like they are with heavier fixed dumbbells, so recovery-focused users get genuine value here.

What's the real difference between the 560 and the 1090 model?

Weight range. The 560 maxes out at 25 pounds; the 1090 goes to 90 pounds. The 1090 is also bulkier and costs significantly more. Choose the 560 if 25 pounds is adequate for your strongest lifts. If you're already pressing or rowing above that weight, the 1090 is worth the upgrade. Most intermediate lifters find the 1090 unnecessary.
