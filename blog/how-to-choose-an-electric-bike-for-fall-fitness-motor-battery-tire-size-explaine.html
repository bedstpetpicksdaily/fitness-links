Motor Power: How Many Watts Do You Actually Need?

Motor output is the spec most shoppers fixate on, and it's the one most often misunderstood. A 250W to 500W hub motor is plenty for flat urban commuting and paved bike paths — it will get you to 20 mph comfortably and sip battery while doing it. Where you need more is on hills, in headwinds, or when you're pushing through wet leaves, packed sand or gravel. That's when a 750W or 1000W motor with strong peak torque stops feeling like a luxury and starts feeling like the difference between finishing your loop and turning back early. Burchda Bikes builds most of its fat tire and all-terrain models around high-torque rear hub motors in that 750W+ range, which is the sweet spot for riders who want fitness benefits without getting stranded halfway up a climb. Look at torque figures (measured in Nm) as much as wattage — torque is what gets you moving from a stop and up a grade, and anything in the 80Nm range will handle serious inclines.

Battery Capacity and Cold-Weather Range

Here's the fall reality nobody mentions in the spec sheet: lithium-ion batteries lose efficiency in cold weather. A pack that delivers 50 miles at 75°F might give you 35 to 40 at 40°F. That means the battery you buy for autumn and winter riding should be oversized relative to your typical route. Look for capacity measured in amp-hours and volts — a 48V 15Ah pack (720Wh) is a solid benchmark for genuine long-range riding, while smaller 10Ah packs suit short commutes. Burchda Bikes offers models with large-capacity removable batteries, which is a genuinely important feature in cold months: being able to pull the pack out and charge it indoors at room temperature protects battery health and gives you a full-strength start every ride. Also check whether the frame supports a second battery or has an easy swap system if you're planning long weekend rides in the hills.

Tire Size: Why Fat Tires Make Sense in Fall

Tire choice is where fall riding really separates itself from summer. Standard 1.95" to 2.4" commuter tires are fast and efficient on dry pavement, but they get twitchy on wet leaves and painted road markings, and they sink in soft ground. Fat tires — typically 4" wide on 20" or 26" rims — run at much lower pressures, which spreads your contact patch, adds grip and delivers natural suspension over roots, potholes and frost-heaved pavement. The trade-off is rolling resistance and weight, which is exactly why a strong motor pairs so well with them. Burchda's fat tire electric bikes lean into this combination: wide, aggressive-tread tires matched to high-torque motors, so you get all-season traction without feeling like you're dragging an anchor. If your riding is 90% smooth pavement, a 2.4" commuter tire on a folding Burchda model will be lighter and quicker. If you're mixing in gravel paths, canal towpaths, beach or forest trails, go fat.

Matching the Bike to Your Fall Fitness Goal

Be honest about how you'll use it. If your goal is replacing car trips and getting daily movement, a folding commuter e-bike with a mid-range battery, fenders and integrated lights is the practical pick — it stores in an apartment, fits in a car boot and handles wet mornings. If your goal is cardio volume and weekend adventure, prioritise a fat tire model with the biggest battery you can afford and a torque-rich motor. Either way, use the lowest pedal assist level that keeps you moving comfortably; that's how you turn an e-bike into genuine training rather than a scooter. Riders who drop to PAS 1 or 2 and let their legs do most of the work regularly log more weekly minutes on an e-bike than they ever did on an analogue bike, simply because they ride more often. Frequently Asked Questions

Do you still get a workout on an electric bike?

Yes — pedal assist scales the help you receive, so riding in the lowest assist levels keeps your heart rate in a genuine training zone. Most riders find they log more total weekly minutes on an e-bike because hills, headwinds and long return legs stop being deterrents.

How much does cold weather reduce e-bike battery range?

Expect roughly a 20–30% drop in range once temperatures fall near freezing. Storing and charging the battery indoors at room temperature, then fitting it just before you ride, recovers a large part of that loss — which is why removable packs like those on Burchda models are worth prioritising.

Are fat tires better than standard tires for fall and winter riding?

Generally yes. Four-inch fat tires run at low pressure, spreading your contact patch for better grip on wet leaves, gravel, mud and light snow, and they cushion rough pavement. The trade-off is extra weight and rolling resistance, so pair them with a 750W or higher motor.
