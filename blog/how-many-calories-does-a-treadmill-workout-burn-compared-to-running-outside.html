The Short Answer

Treadmill running burns fewer calories than outdoor running at identical speeds due to reduced air resistance and the motorized belt doing some of the work. The difference averages 5-10% fewer calories burned on a treadmill. You can offset this by adding an incline of 1-2% to your treadmill workout. For most people, the convenience and consistency of treadmill training outweigh the slightly lower calorie burn.

The Full Explanation

Understanding the calorie difference between treadmill and outdoor running requires examining the biomechanics of each activity. When you run outside, your body must propel itself forward against wind resistance and varying terrain. You're also responsible for pushing off the ground with each stride, engaging stabilizer muscles throughout your core, legs, and feet. The treadmill, by contrast, has a motorized belt that moves beneath you, reducing the muscular effort required for forward propulsion.

Research published in the Journal of Sports Sciences found that outdoor running at a given speed requires approximately 5% more energy expenditure than treadmill running. Some studies suggest the difference can reach up to 10% depending on speed, outdoor conditions, and individual running form. A runner working at 10 mph on a treadmill might burn around 680 calories per hour, while the same person running outdoors could burn 750+ calories in the same timeframe.

Several factors contribute to this calorie deficit on treadmills:

  • Wind resistance: Outside, you push through air resistance with every stride. On a treadmill, you're stationary relative to your surroundings.
  • Ground impact variability: Outdoor surfaces (asphalt, grass, trails) vary slightly with each step, requiring constant micro-adjustments. Treadmills provide consistent, predictable surfaces.
  • Motor assistance: The belt does some of the work pulling your legs back, whereas outside you must actively drive each stride.
  • Stabilizer muscle engagement: Outdoor running demands more activation of balance and stabilization muscles to maintain form on uneven terrain.

The good news: you can eliminate this difference entirely. Adding a 1-2% incline to your treadmill workout compensates for wind resistance and the motorized belt, bringing your calorie burn in line with outdoor running. Many fitness experts recommend a 1% incline as the baseline for any treadmill running workout to simulate real-world conditions.

What the Experts Say

The American College of Sports Medicine acknowledges the calorie differential between treadmill and outdoor running. Sports physiologist Dr. Jack Daniels recommends that runners training on treadmills add a 1% grade to match outdoor effort levels. Fitness coaches widely agree that treadmill running remains an excellent cardiovascular workout, especially for building aerobic base and maintaining consistent training intensity. The convenience factor often outweighs the calorie differential—a treadmill workout you actually complete burns more calories than an outdoor run you skip due to weather or scheduling.
