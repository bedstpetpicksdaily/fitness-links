Concept2 Model D vs Model E Rowing Machine: Which Should You Actually Buy? (2026)

Photo: Victor Freitas / Pexels

By Best Fitness Picks Daily • July 20, 2026 • Contains affiliate links

Concept2 dominates the home rowing machine market for a reason: their machines are used in CrossFit boxes, university rowing programs, and serious home gyms worldwide. But the brand offers multiple models at different price points, and choosing between them requires understanding what actually changes—and what doesn't—as you move up the product line.

Both the Model D and Model E deliver full-body workouts engaging 85%+ of your muscles with low-impact, high-intensity training. Both feature Concept2's respected PM5 monitor and damper resistance systems. The key differences lie in resistance technology, footprint, durability trajectory, and total cost of ownership. This guide breaks down what you're actually paying for and which machine makes sense for your setup.

Concept2 Model D

The Model D is Concept2's premium flagship, measuring 96 inches long by 24 inches wide, with a sealed chain drive and fluid damper system. It folds vertically to approximately 14×24 inches for storage. The PM5 monitor tracks splits, pace, calories, and power output with precision-grade accuracy (to the tenth of a second), syncing with Concept2's online logbook for multi-month progress tracking. Price range: $900–$1,200 depending on PM5 configuration.

Best for: Competitive rowers, serious athletes, CrossFit users, and buyers prioritizing longevity and resale value over initial cost

Concept2 Model E

The Model E uses a magnetic resistance system instead of fluid damping, operating virtually silently with zero maintenance required. It's more compact (roughly 8 feet long, 2 feet wide) and folds for apartment-friendly storage. Like the Model D, it includes the PM5 monitor with competitive rowing metrics, but the magnetic system requires no belt adjustments or resistance degradation management. Price positioning appears slightly lower than the Model D based on source material references.

Best for: Apartment dwellers, noise-sensitive users, buyers seeking maintenance-free operation, and those wanting proven durability without the fluid damper learning curve

Budget Magnetic Rowers (sub-$600 alternatives)

Source material references budget models under $600 with basic monitors and magnetic resistance systems. These machines typically sound like grinding metal after 3 months, degrade in resistance quality, and hold little resale value. They lack the precision tracking and durability of Concept2 models, often becoming unused gym equipment within months.

Best for: First-time buyers testing whether they'll actually stick with rowing before committing to premium equipment

WaterRower (premium alternative brand mentioned)

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the actual difference between Concept2 Model D and Model E resistance systems?

Model D uses a fluid damper (air/water-like feel, requires damper adjustments 1–10, most realistic rowing sensation). Model E uses magnetic resistance (silent, zero maintenance, consistent feel without damper settings). Neither system degrades significantly over time; the choice is feel preference and noise tolerance.

Is the PM5 monitor worth the upgrade over basic displays?

Yes, if you do interval training or track progress long-term. The PM5 provides split-time precision (to 0.1 seconds), watts output, stroke rate, and syncs with Concept2's online logbook for competitive rowing programs. Budget monitors show calories and distance—vanity metrics. Concept2 PM5 gives actual rowing data used in collegiate and Olympic programs.

How much space does each model require, and can it fold for apartments?

Model D: 96 inches long × 24 inches wide unfolded; folds to ~14×24 inches. Model E: ~8 feet long × 2 feet wide unfolded; also folds for compact storage. Both fit apartments, but Model E has slightly tighter footprint. Vertical folding on both saves floor space significantly.

How often does a Concept2 Model D or E need maintenance?

Model D (fluid damper): Minimal—sealed chain drive requires occasional inspection; damper adjustment based on feel preference. Model E (magnetic): Virtually no maintenance required; no belts, no resistance degradation, no adjustments needed. Both machines operate smoothly after 500+ workouts. Compare to budget rowers requiring belt adjustments within 3 months.

What's the realistic resale value if I buy and use for 2–3 years?

Concept2 models (D and E) hold 70–80% of purchase price on the used market after 2–3 years of ownership. Budget rowers ($300–$600) often can't be resold. Over 10 years of use, your true Concept2 cost-per-use becomes competitive with or cheaper than initial budget purchase price.

Can beginners use the Model D or E, or are they only for experienced rowers?

Both are beginner-friendly. Adjustable damper settings (Model D) and magnetic resistance (Model E) allow beginners to start at lower intensity without feeling overwhelmed on day one. Advanced rowers won't plateau because settings scale up. Model E's magnetic system may feel slightly more forgiving for form learning.
