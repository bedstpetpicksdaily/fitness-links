Our Verdict

The Bowflex SelectTech 560 deserves its 4.3-star rating, but it's best suited for small-space home gyms or solo trainers who don't need multiple kettlebells simultaneously. If you're building a functional fitness setup and already have dumbbells, skip this—grab two or three traditional kettlebells instead at half the cost. But if square footage is your limiting factor and you swing kettlebells 2-3 times weekly as part of a balanced routine, the convenience and build quality justify the investment. Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Bowflex SelectTech 560 handle explosive movements like kettlebell snatches?

It performs adequately for snatches, but not perfectly. The weight distribution at heavier settings (35-40 lbs) feels slightly different than traditional cast iron kettlebells—the mass sits slightly forward on the handle. For pure explosive work, a traditional kettlebell offers more authentic movement mechanics. That said, the 10-20 pound range feels nearly identical to standard kettlebells, so lighter snatch work translates directly.

Can you use two SelectTech 560s for ballistic movements, or is the price too high?

Technically yes, but I wouldn't recommend it as a primary strategy. Two units would run $600-800+ depending on sales, which is the cost of 4-6 quality traditional kettlebells in varied weights. If you have the budget and space constraints are critical, it works. Most home gym builders are better served buying one SelectTech and 2-3 traditional kettlebells in complementary weights.

How does this compare to adjustable dumbbell sets for the same price?

Different tools for different goals. Adjustable dumbbells offer more exercise variety (benching, isolation work, unilateral movements), while the SelectTech 560 specializes in kettlebell-specific patterns (swings, Turkish get-ups, carries). If you're doing primarily kettlebell training, this makes sense. If you're building a balanced home gym, an adjustable dumbbell set typically delivers more bang for the same investment.
