Our Verdict

The Matador Short delivers real value if you're space-constrained, rent your home, or need something that takes literally five seconds to deploy for a quick morning set. Frequently Asked Questions

Will this bar work on my apartment doorframe if it's metal?

Yes, but with a caveat. Metal doorframes grip the bar differently than wood, and slipping has been reported by apartment dwellers in reviews. The fix: use rubber grip pads (cheap, under $10) on the bar's feet or stick the included foam grips on contact points. Most users who added padding reported zero slipping afterward.

Can I do weighted pull-ups or just bodyweight?

Bodyweight and light resistance bands are safe. At your body weight plus a 5-10 pound resistance band, you're fine. If you're planning to hang a 25-pound weight plate from your waist, this isn't engineered for that—the door hinge system isn't load-rated the same way a wall-mounted bar is. Stick with resistance bands for added difficulty instead.

How does this compare to a resistance band for pull-ups?

Completely different tools. Resistance bands are cheaper (often $15-30), take up no space, and travel anywhere. But they don't replicate true pull-ups—the resistance changes throughout the movement, and you never achieve full dead-hang strength. The Matador costs more but gives you actual pull-up strength development. Pick bands if you want convenience and low cost; pick the Matador if you want measurable upper-body strength gains without installing permanent hardware.

Is the 'portable' claim real or just marketing?

Real, but with realistic expectations. You can move it between rooms in seconds, which is genuinely convenient for shared spaces. You won't be throwing it in a gym bag and flying across the country with it. It weighs 8 pounds and takes up doorframe space, so it's 'portable' in the sense of 'not permanently mounted'—not 'travel-friendly like a resistance band.' That portability matters for renters or people who rearrange furniture, less so for permanent home gym setups.
