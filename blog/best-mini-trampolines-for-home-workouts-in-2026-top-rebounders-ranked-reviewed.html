Why Rebounding Is the Smartest Home Cardio of 2026

Rebounding works because every bounce puts your entire body through a rapid cycle of acceleration and deceleration. That means your muscles, from your calves to your deep core stabilisers, engage on every single rep — yet the trampoline mat absorbs the impact your joints would otherwise take on pavement. Research consistently shows rebounding can match jogging for cardiovascular benefit at a lower perceived exertion, which is why so many people find they can stick with it. Add in the balance, coordination and lymphatic circulation benefits, and it's easy to see why rebounder sales have climbed year after year. For anyone in a flat, a small house or a shared building, the low noise and tiny footprint are the clinchers.

What to Look For Before You Buy

Prioritise bungee or elastic-rope suspension over steel springs for a quieter, lower-impact bounce. Check the stated user weight limit and make sure it comfortably exceeds your own — a higher limit usually indicates a stronger frame. If you're new to rebounding, over 50, or training on a hard floor, get a model with a stability bar; it's the single best safety upgrade you can make. Consider your storage situation too: folding legs make a huge difference in a flat. Finally, factor in mat diameter — around 40 inches suits most adults for full cardio routines, while smaller mats suit tighter spaces but limit lateral movement.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I bounce on a mini trampoline each day?

Most beginners start with 10 to 15 minutes a day and build up to 20 to 30 minutes, three to five times a week. Because rebounding is low-impact, you can safely do it more frequently than running — just listen to your body and increase gradually.

Is a rebounder good for weight loss?

Yes. A moderate-to-vigorous rebounding session can burn a comparable number of calories to jogging while feeling easier on the joints, and it engages the core, glutes and legs throughout. Combined with sensible nutrition, it's an effective and sustainable fat-loss tool.

Do I need a mini trampoline with a handlebar?

If you're new to rebounding, returning from injury, over 50, or simply want extra confidence during faster routines, a handlebar is well worth it. Boogie Bounce models include an adjustable stability bar, and you can always train without holding it as your balance improves.
