Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ Band pegs are legitimately useful for progressive training. Unlike basic doorway bars that only offer bodyweight, the integrated pegs let you loop resistance bands for assisted pull-ups, eccentric training, and mobility work. This single feature extends the bar's lifespan from 'beginner tool' to 'intermediate training station.'
  • ✓ Doorway installation requires zero tools or permanent damage. The tension-based design grips both sides of your frame, meaning you can move it between rooms or take it with you if you relocate.   • ✓ Weight capacity is legit at 300+ lbs. You're not bottlenecked by the equipment if you're serious about progression. Plenty of doorway bars top out at 250 lbs, making the Matador a better long-term investment if you plan on weighted pull-ups down the line.

Cons
  • ✗ The price sits in an awkward middle zone. You're paying premium-adjacent pricing ($100-150 range depending on sales) for a doorway bar when models like the basic Chin-Up Max cost $40-60. The band pegs add value, but not $60-90 worth of value for most casual lifters who'll do pull-ups once or twice weekly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will this damage my doorframe?

No, assuming your door frame is standard wood construction (which 99% of interior doors are). The tension-based mounting system distributes pressure across the frame without requiring drilling or permanent installation. That said, if you have a very thin, weak, or damaged frame, you might want to test it on a secondary door first. The bar grips both sides of the frame, so the clamping force is distributed rather than concentrated at one point.

How does the Matador Pro Plus compare to cheaper doorway bars like the Iron Gym or Chin-Up Max?

The core pull-up function is identical across all three—they all use tension-based mounting and support basic pull-ups. The Matador Pro Plus differentiator is the band pegs. If you plan to use resistance bands for assisted pull-ups, eccentric training, or mobility work, the Matador is worth the premium. If you just want to do straight pull-ups or chin-ups, the $40-60 alternatives are honestly fine and save you real money. The Rogue brand name carries weight in fitness circles, but that reputation isn't free—you're paying for it.

Can this hold my weight if I'm over 250 lbs?

Yes, the Matador Pro Plus supports 300+ lbs according to specs, which puts it above many doorway alternatives. However, I'd recommend testing it on your specific door frame first. Weight capacity is one thing; your frame's structural integrity is another. If your door is in an older home or feels flimsy, start with slower, controlled movements before attempting heavy eccentric pull-ups or weighted dips. Most failures happen during sudden movements, not static holds.
