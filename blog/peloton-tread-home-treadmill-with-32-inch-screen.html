Frequently Asked Questions

Do you need the Peloton subscription to use the Tread+?

Technically no—the machine works as a standard treadmill without the subscription. But that's like buying a smartphone and not paying for internet. The classes and community features are why people actually use this equipment consistently. Without the subscription, you're paying premium price for basic functionality. Most owners treat the $44/month subscription as part of the cost, not an optional add-on.

How does the Peloton Tread+ compare to regular treadmills or NordicTrack?

The key difference is instructor consistency and form feedback. NordicTrack has interactive maps and incline features that adjust automatically, which is cool for variety. Peloton's strength is the live and on-demand classes with real instructors cueing your form—that accountability makes people actually use it. If you want scenic runs and max customization, NordicTrack wins. If you want structure and motivation, Peloton wins. The 29 verified reviews lean heavily toward people who prioritize the instructor experience.

Is the 32-inch screen worth the extra cost, or can I watch classes on a tablet instead?

You can absolutely watch on a tablet, but the experience is dramatically different. The 32-inch built-in screen puts the instructor at eye level, makes form cues visible without turning your head, and keeps you engaged during hard intervals when your attention is fragile. Using a tablet feels like watching fitness content. Using the screen feels like being in a class. If screen size matters to your motivation, it's worth it. If you're someone who needs the smallest price point, skip the Tread+ and get a basic treadmill plus a tablet.

How much space do you actually need, and does it fit in apartments?

The Tread+ footprint is 71L x 36W inches—that's roughly the size of a king bed. In most apartments, this means either your bedroom, living room, or dedicated home office. You need clear space around it for safety, so don't plan on squeezing it into a corner. If you live in under 800 square feet, this is a serious space decision. Measure your actual available floor space first. No amount of motivation fixes a machine that doesn't fit.
