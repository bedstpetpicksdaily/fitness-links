Frequently Asked Questions

Are fabric resistance bands better than latex for glute workouts?

Fabric resistance bands are generally superior for glute training because they stay in place during movement, don't roll or slip, and provide a more comfortable grip against skin. Latex bands can roll up during exercises like lateral walks and donkey kicks, reducing glute activation and causing discomfort. Fabric bands also tend to be more durable and won't degrade from sweat and friction.

What resistance level should I choose for glute exercises?

Most beginners should start with light to medium resistance (typically 15-30 lbs of equivalent resistance) to master form on exercises like glute bridges and lateral band walks. As you progress, move to heavy resistance (40+ lbs) for compound movements like banded squats and hip thrusts to continue building strength and muscle. The right level allows you to complete 12-15 reps with the last 2-3 reps being challenging.

Do fabric resistance bands really activate glutes better than free weights?

Fabric bands provide constant tension throughout the entire range of motion, which can actually increase glute activation compared to free weights where tension decreases at the bottom of movements. They're excellent for activation and hypertrophy, but most strength coaches recommend combining them with free weights rather than choosing one over the other for optimal glute development. Bands are particularly effective for isolation exercises and targeting weak points in your range of motion.

How do I keep fabric resistance bands from rolling during workouts?

Quality fabric bands have a textured, non-slip surface specifically designed to stay in place during lateral movements and exercises. For extra grip, wear fitted leggings rather than loose pants, and position the band high on your thighs just above the knees for lateral exercises. If your bands consistently slip, they may be lower quality or stretched out—consider replacing them with a premium brand designed for glute training.
