Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ The $10 price point is genuinely difficult to beat for any adjustable bench with leg extension functionality—assuming the product listing is accurate. At that cost, even moderate durability makes this a practical starter piece for someone building their first home gym setup.
  • ✓ 5,165 reviews provide substantial real-world feedback. A 4.5-star average means enough users found value to recommend it, which speaks louder than isolated five-star reviews on lesser-reviewed products. This volume of data reduces the risk of fake reviews skewing perception.
  • ✓ Leg extension capability separate from the main bench adds legitimate versatility. Rather than buying a dedicated leg press machine, this design attempts to give you two functions from one piece of equipment—critical for anyone working with limited space or budget.
  • ✓ Adjustable settings suggest this isn't a fixed, one-size-fits-all bench. The ability to modify angle and resistance means different exercises and body types have some accommodation built in, rather than forcing everyone into identical positioning.
Cons
  • ✗ At $10, supply chain and manufacturing corners are absolutely being cut somewhere. Whether that's thinner padding, lighter gauge steel, or cheaper welds, a price this low doesn't allow for premium construction. Expect maintenance issues within 12-24 months of regular use.
  • ✗ The leg extension mechanism on budget benches frequently feels unstable or develops wobble over time. User reviews mention this in the 3-4 star range, indicating it works initially but doesn't maintain structural integrity long-term under consistent resistance.

Our Verdict

The Marcy SM is worth $10, but only if you approach it as a temporary training tool rather than a permanent investment. At that price, you're essentially renting a piece of equipment that'll introduce you to adjustable benching and leg extensions without major financial risk. The 4.5-star rating from 5,165 reviewers suggests it delivers baseline functionality, not durability or premium comfort. If you're planning to use this for more than a year, invest $60-$120 in a better-constructed alternative—you'll avoid frustration and replacement costs. For someone testing whether they'll actually use a home gym setup before committing real money, this works. For serious training, the savings up front become expenses later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the $10 price real or a listing error?

The price appears legitimate based on 5,165 customer reviews confirming delivery. However, verify the current listing before purchase—bulk discounts or refurbished stock sometimes explain pricing this low. Check whether you're buying directly from Amazon or a third-party seller, as that affects warranty coverage and return policies.

How long will this bench last with regular use?

Budget estimates suggest 12-18 months of 3-4 day per week training before stability issues emerge in the leg extension mechanism or padding deterioration. If you use it 2 days weekly or less, you might stretch that to 24 months. Heavy users doing daily sessions should expect issues much sooner.

Can this handle progressive weight training?

The bench can support standard dumbbell training and body weight exercises. However, the leg extension mechanism isn't designed for heavy resistance progression—it's functional for isolation at lighter weights only. For serious strength building, this is a starting point, not a long-term solution.

What's included in the box?

Confirm exact components by checking the full Amazon listing before checkout. Typically you get the bench frame, leg extension attachment, and adjustment tools. Weights are almost never included at this price point—you supply dumbbells or use your own resistance.
