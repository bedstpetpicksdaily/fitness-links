Frequently Asked Questions

What thickness should a yoga mat have for extra cushioning?

For maximum cushioning, look for yoga mats between 6-8mm thick, which provide superior joint protection compared to standard 3-5mm mats. Thicker mats are ideal if you have sensitive knees, wrists, or practice on hard floors like tile or concrete. However, anything over 8mm may reduce stability for balance poses like Tree Pose or Warrior III.

Are thick yoga mats good for all types of yoga?

Thick cushioned mats work best for gentle, restorative, and yin yoga practices where comfort is prioritized. For high-intensity styles like Vinyasa or power yoga, thicker mats can reduce stability and make transitions feel unstable. Consider a 5-6mm mat as a compromise if you practice multiple yoga styles.

What material is best for a thick cushioned yoga mat?

TPE (thermoplastic elastomer) and PVC are the most durable materials for thick yoga mats, offering excellent cushioning and longevity. Natural rubber mats also provide great cushioning but are heavier and more expensive, though they're eco-friendly. Avoid foam-only mats as they compress quickly and lose cushioning after a few months.

Will a thick yoga mat affect my balance and stability?

Thicker mats (7-8mm) can slightly reduce stability during balancing poses because your feet sink deeper into the material, making it harder to feel the floor. If balance is important to your practice, choose a denser cushioned mat rather than a thicker one, or stick with 5-6mm thickness. Look for mats with a non-slip textured surface to compensate for reduced ground feedback.
