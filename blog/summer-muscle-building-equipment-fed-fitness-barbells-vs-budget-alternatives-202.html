Frequently Asked Questions

What weight should a beginner start with?

Beginners typically start with an unloaded Olympic barbell (45 pounds) for upper body pressing and pulling movements, and 65-95 pounds for lower body compounds like squats. Add weight in 5-10 pound increments weekly as strength builds. Calibrated weight plates across all price tiers support this progression.

How often should I train to build muscle in summer?

Most effective programs train 4-5 days per week, focusing on compound movements (squats, deadlifts, presses, rows) that engage multiple muscle groups. Frequency matters less than consistency and progressive overload—training the same weight repeatedly produces no muscle growth.

What's the actual difference between FED Fitness and budget barbells?

FED Fitness uses higher-grade steel with tighter engineering tolerances, meaning sleeves spin smoother, knurling lasts longer, and the barbell maintains specifications through years of heavy use. Budget barbells function adequately initially but degrade faster under load. For casual summer training, budget works; for serious long-term training, mid-range or premium justify the cost.

Can I build muscle with just barbells and plates?

Yes. Barbells enable all essential compound movements (squats, deadlifts, bench press, overhead press, rows) that drive muscle growth. You don't need machines, dumbbells, or accessories—progressive overload with compound movements is sufficient. Quality plates in precise increments matter more than brand prestige.

How do I prevent injury during intense summer training?

Start with weights you can control for 8-12 reps with good form. Increase load only when the previous weight becomes easy. Quality knurling (especially on premium bars) prevents grip slippage during heavy lifts. Use a power rack with safety bars for squats and bench press. Warm up properly before heavy sets. Injury prevention is 80% form and programming, 20% equipment quality.
