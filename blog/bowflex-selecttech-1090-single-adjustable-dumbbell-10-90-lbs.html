Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ Weight range of 10-90 lbs in 5 lb increments means you can genuinely replace multiple dumbbells without buying a full set—realistic space saver for apartments or small homes
  • ✓ Dial selector is genuinely fast to use between sets; switching from 35 to 50 lbs takes about 3 seconds, which keeps your workout flowing instead of fumbling with collars and plates

Cons
  • ✗ The price tag ($350-450) requires justification; you could buy 3-4 complete adjustable dumbbell sets for the same cost if you're setting up a full home gym with pairs
  • ✗ Dial mechanism requires occasional maintenance—one reviewer mentioned sticky selection after 18 months of heavy use, and replacement parts aren't cheap or immediately available at local fitness stores

Our Verdict

The Bowflex SelectTech 1090 is worth it if you're a solo lifter who trains at home and genuinely can't spare the space for multiple dumbbells or a full rack. If you're the only person using your home gym and you don't need pairs for bilateral exercises like chest presses, this single dumbbell earns its $350-450 price through genuine convenience and durability. However, if you're building a gym for two people, or if you train partners who need simultaneous access to weights, you'll be frustrated buying this instead of a pair of adjustables. The 4.3-star rating reflects real user satisfaction, but that includes reviewers with deeper budgets and smaller spaces than average. For July 2026 back-to-school fitness season when people are retrofitting home spaces, this makes sense only if your budget is fixed and your space is tight.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use one Bowflex 1090 for bench press and other bilateral movements?

Technically yes, but awkwardly. You'd hold it in one hand while the other hand grips the bench or floor for stability. Most people switch to lighter dumbbells or adjust their form. If you regularly do bilateral pressing movements, buy a pair instead—this single dumbbell shines for unilateral work like single-arm rows, landmine presses, and offset loaded carries where you want 90 lbs in one hand.

How does it compare to buying separate Bowflex pairs at different weights?

Buying two SelectTech 552s (adjustable up to 52.5 lbs each) costs roughly the same as one 1090, but gives you paired dumbbells for bilateral work. The 1090 is single-only, so it's a different use case. If you do regular barbell-style exercises with dumbbells, pairs win. If you do isolation work and single-limb training, the 1090's heavier max weight (90 lbs) becomes the deciding factor.

Is the dial dial mechanism reliable long-term, or does it get stuck?

Out of 500+ reviews, most report solid durability through 2-3 years of regular use. However, a small percentage mention the dial sticking around the 18-month mark, typically after heavy sweaty workouts without cleaning. Wipe it down occasionally and store it in a dry space. Replacement dial kits cost $40-60 if needed, which is still cheaper than buying new dumbbells but annoying to deal with.

What's the real footprint difference between this and a traditional dumbbell set?

One 1090 takes up roughly the space of three pairs of traditional dumbbells (6 dumbbells total). If you have a small apartment or workout corner, that matters. If you already have room for a squat rack or bench, the space savings feel overstated. Measure your actual storage space before deciding—this is marketed as a space-saver but it's really just one dumbbell.
