Frequently Asked Questions

How does the SelectTech 560 compare to the SelectTech 552?

The 560 extends to 560 pounds versus the 552's 52.5 pounds—a massive difference if you're doing heavy compound movements. The 552 suits most home lifters perfectly well and costs less. Upgrade to the 560 only if you're regularly pulling heavy deadlifts, rows, or doing loaded carries. Most people honestly don't need the jump, even if they think they do.

Is the dial adjustment system reliable long-term?

Yes, across hundreds of verified reviews the dial mechanism holds up well beyond three years of regular use. Early reports flagged some wobbling after heavy use, but Bowflex addressed this in later production runs. If you're buying new, you'll get the updated version. The mechanism is mechanical, not electronic, so it doesn't have batteries to die—just basic dial reliability.

Can you do drop sets efficiently with adjustable dumbbells?

Absolutely, and this is where the SelectTech 560 genuinely shines. You can complete an intense drop set in seconds—hit your working weight, rotate the dial, and immediately grab the reduced weight. With traditional dumbbells you'd walk to the rack, swap pairs, and lose momentum. For anyone doing high-intensity training, this matters more than people expect.

What's the actual floor space requirement?

The base footprint is roughly 10 inches wide by 24 inches long. That's smaller than most people assume, but you need clearance around it for safe lifting and the selector dial to rotate. Plan for about 4 feet of usable space per dumbbell in your actual training area, or they'll collect dust in a closet. An 8-foot space handles a pair reasonably well.

Do these dumbbells feel balanced compared to traditional ones?

At lighter weights (under 50 pounds), the balance is excellent. As you move heavier, the adjustment mechanism adds slight front-weight feel compared to solid cast iron. It's not a dealbreaker—you adapt within one workout—but it's noticeable if you're coming from traditional dumbbells. Exercises like dumbbell presses feel slightly different in hand.
