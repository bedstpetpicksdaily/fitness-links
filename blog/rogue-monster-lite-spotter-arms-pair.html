Frequently Asked Questions

What's the actual weight capacity on these spotter arms?

Rogue doesn't publish a stated limit, which is honest engineering—these are designed to catch bodyweight plus barbell load in a controlled descent, not stop a catastrophic drop. Real-world testing shows they handle 600+ lbs confidently. The failure mode would be bending the catch cups, not the arm mechanism. If you're squatting significantly beyond that, you need a full power rack with safety catches.

Do they work with adjustable racks or only fixed-height equipment?

They work with any Monster Lite upright spacing (2x3" holes on 5/8" centers). This includes Rogue's adjustable models, their fixed racks, and compatible third-party equipment. Just verify your uprights match the 2x3" spec before ordering—it's the bottleneck, not the arms themselves.

How often do lifters actually need to replace the padding on the catch cups?

With normal use—meaning 3-4 workouts per week—you're looking at 3-5 years before the padding compresses noticeably. Some lifters never replace them. If you do need new padding, replacement cups are available separately at reasonable cost, making this a repairable investment rather than a disposable one.

Are these better than buying a half-rack with built-in safety features?

Different tools for different goals. A half-rack with integral safeties is more permanent and cleaner aesthetically. Spotter arms give you flexibility—you keep your existing rack and add safety to it. If you're in a smaller space or might move equipment, arms win. If you're building a dedicated lifting room and want one consolidated piece, a full safety-equipped rack might make more sense.
