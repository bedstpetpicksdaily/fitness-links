Frequently Asked Questions

How do PowerBlocks Pro EXP compare to cheaper adjustable dumbbells?

Cheaper adjustable sets ($200-300) use similar expansion-pin technology but often have looser pin connections, weaker urethane coatings, and less refined grip ergonomics. PowerBlocks command a premium largely for build quality consistency and the brand's 30-year track record. If you're buying once and keeping for 5+ years, the durability argument holds up. If you think you'll upgrade within 18-24 months, the cheaper option makes financial sense.

Will these work for bench press and other compound lifts?

Yes, but with caveats. The block shape works fine for pressing movements, but dumbbell rows and unilateral work feel different compared to traditional dumbbells because the weight distribution shifts. Users report needing 2-3 sessions to adjust their form. For isolation work and pressing, no issue. For serious hypertrophy-focused rowing, some lifters prefer traditional dumbbells because of the feel, even if the weight moved is identical.

Is the 5-90 lb range actually enough, or will I outgrow it?

Depends on your lift and timeline. For bench press, 90 lbs per hand gets you to advanced intermediate territory—solid for 2+ years for most lifters. For bicep curls or shoulder work, you'll max out faster. For legs (Bulgarian split squats, goblet squats), the 90 lb cap matters less since you're holding one dumbbell. Be honest about your actual strength trajectory before buying. If you're already pressing 80+ lb dumbbells regularly, PowerBlocks Pro EXP isn't future-proof.
