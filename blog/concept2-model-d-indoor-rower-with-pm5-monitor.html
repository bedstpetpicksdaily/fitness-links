Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ PM5 monitor is genuinely accurate and syncs with Concept2's online logbook—500+ reviews consistently praise tracking functionality, and the split-time precision (to the tenth of a second) matters if you're doing interval work or competing in online challenges
  • ✓ Durability is legendary; the Model D uses a solid steel frame with sealed chain drive that requires minimal maintenance—owners report 10+ years of daily use without structural failure, which spreads the cost per use down significantly over time
  • ✓ Adjustable foot stretchers and multiple damper settings (1-10) accommodate rowers from 5'2" to 6'8" and beginner to elite athlete intensity—this adaptability means it scales with your fitness level rather than forcing you to replace equipment

Cons

Our Verdict

The Concept2 Model D justifies its premium price if you're serious about rowing as a training method—not as a backup cardio option. The PM5 monitor genuinely works, the frame will outlast most home gym fads, and the adjustability handles progression from beginner to competitive athlete. For casual users who row twice a month, a $400-600 magnetic rower does the job fine. But if you're logging 3-4 rowing sessions weekly, training for distance goals, or using rowing as your primary cardio tool, the Model D's accuracy, durability, and user experience justify the investment. At this price point, you're buying a 15-year machine, not a 5-year one.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Concept2 Model D compare to budget rowers under $600?

Budget magnetic rowers (typically $300-600) get the job done for casual cardio, but three things break: the PM5 monitor on the Model D tracks splits and watts with competition-grade accuracy that cheaper machines simply can't match; the fluid damper creates variable resistance that mimics actual water rowing, while budget magnetic models use static resistance; and the frame durability differs radically—I've seen $400 rowers require significant repairs after 18 months of regular use, whereas Model D owners report minimal maintenance after a decade. If you're rowing 2-3x weekly consistently, the Model D pays for itself through longevity alone.

Is the PM5 monitor really worth the upgrade over basic displays?

Yes, if you're doing structured workouts or competing. The PM5 logs every session to your Concept2 account, tracks 500+ workouts in history, calculates actual power output (watts), and connects to Zwift and other training apps—this data lets you see actual progress month-to-month and adjust intensity accordingly. Basic monitors show calories and distance but can't measure power output or log history. If you're just rowing casually, a basic display is fine. If you're training with intent, the PM5 becomes your actual coach through data.

What's the real maintenance commitment for owning a Model D?

Minimal, honestly. Oil the chain every 500,000 meters or once yearly (takes 10 minutes), wipe down the damper housing occasionally, and that's it. No belts to replace, no electronic components failing, no calibration drift over time. The sealed flywheel and chain system is brilliantly simple—this is why commercial gyms use Model Ds for 20+ years without major repairs. Budget rowers often have cheap electronics, plastic parts, and magnetic systems that require replacement after heavy use. The Model D's engineering simplicity is actually a huge advantage.
