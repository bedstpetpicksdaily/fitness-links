Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ The PM5 monitor is genuinely accurate for tracking splits, power output, and calories burned—I verified this against my smartwatch and fitness tracker data over dozens of workouts. The display is clear without being cluttered, and the data syncs reliably to the Concept 2 logbook app, which is essential if you're tracking progress over months.
  • ✓ Build quality is legitimate industrial-grade construction. After three months of consistent use (4-5 times weekly), there's zero squeaking, no loose parts, and the damper adjustment still feels crisp. This is a machine that will outlast most home gym trends—I have no doubt it'll function perfectly in five years.
  • ✓ The flywheel resistance mimics water or air resistance patterns better than magnetic machines in this price range. The rowing stroke feels naturally smooth rather than mechanical. If you've rowed before or care about proper form, this matters. The damper setting (1-10) gives real versatility without feeling gimmicky.
  • ✓ Space efficiency is better than expected for a full-size rowing machine. At 96 inches long, it fits in my garage corner, and the vertical storage position is genuinely stable. Unlike some machines that feel tip-prone when stored upright, this one is rock-solid.
Cons
  • ✗ The price varies significantly, but you're looking at $800-1,200+ depending on sales. That's not pocket change, and the performance difference versus a $300 magnetic rower isn't massive unless you're specifically training for competitive rowing or long-distance endurance work. For casual fitness, it's overkill.
  • ✗ The seat rail can pinch fingers during adjustment if you're not careful—not a dealbreaker, but worth mentioning since I've seen this happen twice with family members trying to modify settings mid-workout. The adjustment mechanism could use a safety redesign.

Our Verdict

The Concept 2 Model D with PM5 is the closest thing to a professional-grade rowing machine you can buy for a home gym. The monitor is accurate, the build quality is legitimate, and the stroke mechanics are superior to cheaper alternatives. But here's where I push back on the hype: unless you're training seriously, planning to use this 4+ times weekly, or genuinely enjoy rowing, you're paying premium dollars for features you won't use. A $400 magnetic rower will get you fit. This one will do it more precisely and last longer. Whether that precision justifies the price difference is entirely dependent on your commitment level. If you're the type to start strong in July then ignore it by September, save your money. If you're genuinely building a rowing habit, this justifies the investment—just barely.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Concept 2 Model D compare to magnetic rowing machines at half the price?

Magnetic machines are quieter and require less maintenance, but the Concept 2's damper system creates a more authentic rowing feel with better resistance curve consistency. For casual fitness, the difference is barely noticeable. For someone doing 5+ workouts weekly or training for distance, the Concept 2 pulls ahead. The PM5 monitor is also more reliable than cheaper machine displays—it won't lag or disconnect.

Is the PM5 monitor worth upgrading from the basic PM4?

Yes, but only if you care about detailed metrics. The PM5 tracks power output in watts, which the PM4 doesn't. If you're just counting calories and splits, PM4 is fine. If you're programming specific power targets (which serious rowers do), PM5 is necessary. The Bluetooth connectivity to the logbook app is also PM5-exclusive and actually useful for tracking long-term progress.

How much space does this actually take up, and is vertical storage really stable?

It measures 96 inches long and about 26 inches wide, so you need a dedicated 8x2 foot area minimum, or you can store it vertically (which brings footprint to roughly 26x20 inches). I've stored it upright for two months with zero tipping incidents. The locking mechanism is solid. The real consideration is noise and vibration during use—it's quieter than a treadmill but louder than stationary bikes. If you have neighbors directly below, this might be an issue.

What's the actual learning curve if I've never rowed before?

Steep. The Concept 2 is unforgiving on poor form, which is actually good long-term but frustrating at first. I'd budget 8-10 workouts to feel comfortable with proper sequencing (legs, back, arms). The included quick-start guide is insufficient—I recommend watching Concept 2's official YouTube videos on technique. If you buy this, budget 30-45 minutes for proper setup and form education, not just assembly.
