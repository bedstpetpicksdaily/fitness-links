Does the iFit subscription feel necessary or is it just a gimmick?

iFit transforms the experience from "running in place" to "running with purpose." With it, you have a personal trainer on your screen. That said, if you're disciplined enough to program your own intervals and hill work, the treadmill works fine standalone. How loud is this machine during early morning or evening workouts?

It's quieter than budget treadmills but audible. At easy running pace, it's a consistent hum—neighbors won't complain through walls, but someone sleeping nearby will notice. At high incline or sprint speeds, the noise increases noticeably. If noise is a dealbreaker, consider if you have a basement or dedicated space away from sleeping areas.

Is the 22-inch screen actually worth the extra cost versus standard displays?

Yes, if you're using iFit classes. The larger screen means you see the trainer's form cues clearly and the route visuals don't feel cramped. For basic stat tracking (pace, distance, calories), a smaller screen works fine. But the immersion of the larger display genuinely impacts engagement—workouts feel less like staring at a dashboard and more like participating in a class.
