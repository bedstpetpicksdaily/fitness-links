Frequently Asked Questions

Will this damage my doorframe or walls?

No permanent damage—the Matador Max uses friction and compression, not bolts or anchors. Your doorframe paint might have slight marks where the pads grip, but you can wipe them clean. Rental-friendly installation is actually its biggest selling point. Just make sure your doorframe is solid wood and not hollow.

How much weight can it actually hold?

Rogue rates it for 300+ lbs safely. Real-world testing shows it handles muscle-ups, heavy weighted vests, and explosive movements without issue. If you weigh over 250 lbs, check your specific doorframe construction, but the bar itself isn't the limiting factor—it's whether your door opening is structurally sound.

Is this better than the regular Matador, or should I save money?

The 'Max Tall' designation means it fits taller doorframes and leaves more headroom for kipping or full range of motion. If you're under 6 feet tall or have standard door heights, the regular Matador does the job at lower cost. The Max Tall is specifically engineered for tall spaces and taller athletes—buy it only if you need those features.

How does it compare to a ceiling-mounted bar?

Ceiling bars are permanent and more stable, but they require drilling into joists, landlord permission, and professional installation. The Matador Max trades the last 10% of stability for complete portability. If you own your home and want maximum durability, ceiling is better. If you want to use it tomorrow without a drill, this bar wins.
