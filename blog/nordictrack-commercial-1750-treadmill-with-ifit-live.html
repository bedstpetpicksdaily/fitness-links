NordicTrack Commercial 1750 Treadmill: Is It Worth It?

By Best Fitness Picks Daily • July 14, 2026 • Contains affiliate links

The NordicTrack Commercial 1750 landed in my home gym in early July, and I'll be honest—the first thing I noticed wasn't the sleek touchscreen or the iFit Live integration. It was the weight. This machine demands respect, and it demands space. But after three weeks of dawn runs, HIIT sessions, and one particularly ambitious hill climb workout at 6:30 AM, I've got real data to share about whether this $1,500+ investment actually delivers.

With over 500 verified reviews averaging 4.3 stars, the Commercial 1750 sits firmly in the "serious equipment" category. The question isn't whether it exists or works—it's whether you actually need what it offers, and whether those features justify the price tag compared to more affordable treadmill alternatives flooding the market right now.

Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ iFit Live integration genuinely changes workout variety—I've done 40+ different trainer-led classes since setup, and the automatic incline adjustment (up to 15%) syncs perfectly with on-screen terrain. No fumbling with buttons mid-workout.
  • ✓ The 22-inch touchscreen is genuinely responsive and doesn't lag, even when switching between programs or checking heart rate data. After two weeks of daily use, zero software glitches. That's not common in this price range.
  • ✓ Deck cushioning reduces joint impact noticeably—I tested this back-to-back with my neighbor's basic $800 treadmill, and my knees appreciate the difference after 5+ mile runs. The FlexSelect deck actually matters.
  • ✓ Connectivity is seamless: Bluetooth audio, music streaming integration, and the ability to save workouts to your profile means your data persists across devices. I can start a workout on the machine and review metrics on my phone later.
Cons
  • ✗ The footprint is legitimately large—64" long means you need dedicated space, not a treadmill you can fold away into a closet. July humidity in my gym space made the machine run slightly warmer than my old model, requiring better ventilation planning.
  • ✗ Incline motor noise is audible during steep climbs above 12%—not a dealbreaker, but if your gym is near bedrooms, the 6 AM sessions might trigger complaints. Quieter than some competitors, but louder than advertised.

Our Verdict

The NordicTrack Commercial 1750 justifies its price if you're committed to structured training with iFit subscriptions and you have space and electrical setup figured out. The 4.3-star rating reflects real quality, and the 500+ reviews show this isn't a niche product—it's proven. For casual joggers wanting a basic treadmill, spend $600-$900 on a simpler model. For serious runners or gym enthusiasts who'll actually use interactive training and incline workouts multiple times weekly, the touchscreen, iFit ecosystem, and deck quality make the premium worthwhile. This is a 5-year machine, not a 2-year regret.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you actually need the iFit Live subscription, or can you use the treadmill without it?

You can use it without iFit—basic preset programs work fine. But honestly, that's like buying a smartphone and never connecting to WiFi. The whole value proposition is iFit. The subscription runs $10-15/month, and the interactive workouts with trainers and automatic incline adjustments are why people buy this model over cheaper alternatives. After three weeks, I've used iFit on roughly 80% of my workouts.

How does the Commercial 1750 compare to the NordicTrack Commercial 2450 or ProForm alternatives?

The 2450 adds a 4-inch larger screen and slightly better cushioning for $400+ more. For most users, the 1750's 22-inch screen is plenty. ProForm equivalents at similar prices ($1,300-$1,600) often have less stable decks and fewer iFit features. The 1750 is the sweet spot—not the bare minimum, not over-engineered. The 4.3-star rating outperforms several pricier competitors.

What's the real setup and delivery situation like?

White-glove assembly is available (recommended—this thing weighs 150+ pounds). Expect 2-3 hours for full setup including electrical outlet placement. I had delivery on a Monday and was running by Wednesday. FedEx/Amazon logistics on heavy fitness equipment varies by region, so budget an extra week if you're not in a major metro area. Delivery fees typically run $100-$200 depending on location.
